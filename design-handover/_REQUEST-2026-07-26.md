## 3 · Pahchan v1 — redefined, and the main deliverable

Face recognition is **parked to v2**. Device enrollment is **dropped entirely** — it assumes you can pre-register a workforce, and a SaaS onboarding unknown SMEs cannot.

### 3.1 · The v1 model

1. Employee logs into the Kartavya app with their own account.
2. Opens the clock in/out screen.
3. Takes a **live selfie** and presses Clock in. Same on Clock out.
4. Punch syncs with GPS coordinates, accuracy, and both device and server timestamps.
5. Org reviews on the portal: the selfie, a map pin, and the two reference photos side by side.

**Verification is human, not algorithmic.** The supervisor compares the punch selfie against the reference pair. This is a **detective** control, not a preventive one — if someone shares their password, a colleague can punch for them, and the selfie catches it *only if a human looks*. That is an accepted trade-off, but it means review has to be a ten-second scan, not a click-into-each-record chore. Design for that.

### 3.2 · Enrollment reference photos — two per employee

Captured when the employee is enrolled. They are the comparison baseline, and they are what makes v2 a drop-in rather than a re-enrollment campaign across every client's workforce.

- **Slot 1** — straight-on, neutral expression, ID-photo framing.
- **Slot 2** — slight angle (15–30°) or noticeably different lighting.
- Both: face ≥40% of frame, no sunglasses, cap or mask, even light, plain background.

Enforce framing **in the capture UI**, not in a policy paragraph nobody reads. Two rather than one because a single unlucky reference makes every later punch look wrong, and the reviewer stops trusting the comparison.

**Two upload paths, both needed:** HR uploads during employee creation (identity verified at hiring), *or* the employee self-captures on first app run if slots are empty. Self-captured photos land as `pending` in an HR approval queue.

**Never block clock-in on reference photos existing.** Day one of every deployment would be broken. Flag the punch as *"no reference on file"* in the review queue instead.

### 3.3 · Capture must be camera-only

The single most important implementation detail, and it needs to be visible in the design: **in-app camera, no gallery path, no gallery permission requested at all.**

With login-only auth the selfie is the only identity evidence there is. If it can come from the gallery, one saved selfie works forever and the feature is decorative. `07:138` already specifies `CameraView expo-camera, immersive` — that is right, and it must not soften to an image picker.

### 3.4 · Image compression

Minimum storage, but unambiguously clear enough for a human to verify a face.

| | Long edge | Quality | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Punch selfie | 720 px | JPEG q75 | ≤ 100 KB |
| Reference photo | 1080 px | JPEG q85 | ≤ 250 KB |

References get the larger budget because they double as the future embedding source. At 1080 px with a face filling 40% of frame you get roughly 430 px of face, comfortably above what embedding models need. Punch selfies only ever need to satisfy a human eye.

For scale: a 200-worker client generates ~8,800 punch photos a month, about 700 MB, roughly 2.1 GB at steady state under 90-day retention. Reference photos are ~100 MB and permanent. Modest.

### 3.5 · Retention — two different classes

| | Retention |
|---|---|
| Reference photos | Employment duration + **45-day grace** after exit |
| Punch selfies | **90 days** |

Both are policy fields with those defaults. The punch *record* must survive its photo's deletion — hours worked is a payroll fact, the photo is only evidence. A face photo is not a statutory payroll record and should not inherit multi-year HR retention.

### 3.6 · Who can see what

- **Photos** — HR and the `reporting_to` chain only. Never org-wide. `manav_employees.reporting_to` already exists, so this is enforceable today.
- **Aekam (platform surface)** — the **count** of Pahchan users per org. Nothing else. No names, no photos, no locations, no times.

That second one is the mirror of an existing constraint. Margin and cost data must never serialize outside the platform surface; employee attendance data must never serialize *into* it. Both directions, one rule: the platform operator sees aggregates, the org sees its people.

### 3.7 · Automatic reports

Daily, weekly and monthly clock in/out reports, generated and sent automatically to **the HR-assigned user and the org admin**.

**Reports carry no photos.** Times, hours, flags, exceptions, and totals only. Email is not a controlled channel — it forwards, it sits in inboxes indefinitely, it leaves your retention policy behind. Photos stay behind portal authentication where the 90-day rule can actually be enforced. Please design the report layout on that basis.

The backend already has `scheduler.py`, `report_generator.py`, `employee_email.py` and an `OUTBOUND_MODE` kill switch, so this is mostly wiring. What is needed from you is the report layout, the email template, and the policy UI for choosing recipients and cadence.

### 3.8 · Packaging

Pahchan is available from the **Growth tier and above**, and can also be **bought separately** as a standalone add-on.

**Seats are set per org, manually, by Aekam at org creation** — `organisations.max_users`, from migration `061`. The plan's `max_users` is only a fallback default; the org-level figure is what governs, and it is now *enforced* (`ca896ec`), not decorative.

This has a design consequence worth handling deliberately. A client with forty field workers needs forty accounts to punch, so whoever creates that org has to set the allowance accordingly — and if they do not, worker forty-one simply cannot be created. Two things follow:

1. **The org create/edit screen** (`11-platform-admin.md`, `AdminOrgsPage.jsx`) should make the seat figure a deliberate decision rather than a field that gets skipped, and should signal when Pahchan is enabled that attendance users count against it.
2. **The seat-limit error must name the limit and the remedy.** An HR admin hitting the ceiling needs to read "this organisation is allowed N users — contact Aekam to raise it", not a generic failure. Silent or vague failure here reads as a bug in attendance.

**No pricing numbers anywhere in the design.** Not in the prototype, not in the spec, not on the landing page, not as placeholder copy. Tier *names* are fine; figures are not. Standalone Pahchan pricing is set outside the design and blocks none of this.

### 3.9 · Screens requested

1. **Enrollment photo capture** — two-slot UI with framing guides, HR-upload path, employee self-capture path, and the HR approval queue for self-captured photos.
2. **Clock in/out screen** (mobile) — camera-first. Status chips become **GPS · camera · connection**. The `face` chip from `07:77` is gone.
3. **Register view as the primary review surface** — punch selfie beside both references, map pin, day view, team filter. Promoted out of `ExceptionsTab`; exceptions become a filter on top, not the only place photos live.
4. **Consent copy** — photo and location capture, who sees it, how long it is kept. Word it so that enabling v2 face matching does not require re-collecting consent, or state plainly that it will.
5. **Policy fields** — the two retention windows, report recipients and cadence, geofence radius, grace minutes, shift windows.
6. **Two-tab minimal shell** for attendance-only users — Clock and Me. One app, not two: if a user's only entitlement is Pahchan, the app opens straight onto the clock screen. This touches `17-mobile-app.md` as well as `07`.

**Drop from `07`:** device enrollment screens, the face status chip. Keep face matching documented as v2, parked, with the reference-photo pair noted as its enrollment input.

**Also update the punch contract in `07:184`:** `selfie_key` becomes required, `face_score` is dropped, and `device_id` stays but is advisory only — still a useful anomaly signal ("this account punched from three devices today"), no longer an auth factor.

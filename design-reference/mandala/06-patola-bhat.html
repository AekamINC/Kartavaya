पटोला भात — six bhat, drawn on the thread

The earlier patola was wrong in a way worth naming: I faked the feathered edge by nudging points off a clean diamond. That is not what a feathered edge is. Double-ikat is dyed before it is woven, so every colour boundary has to land on a warp and a weft — the edge is not blurred, it is quantised to the thread. So these are built on a real grid, every motif rasterised onto it, and the stepping comes out of the construction rather than being applied to it. Nothing here can draw a diagonal that isn't a staircase, which is exactly the constraint the loom imposes.

Six bhat

All on a 44-thread grid, three tonal levels the way a patola carries three dye passes. They weave in row by row — a cloth appears one pick at a time, and so do these.

The same bhat at three counts

Thread count is the tuning dial. Coarse reads at a glance and survives being shrunk to an inline spinner; fine is a watermark you can put a whole page on top of.

What to know before picking

Ratanchowk is the safest — concentric jewel-squares are the bhat most people picture when they picture patola, and it stays readable at any count.

Vohra Gaji is the one to use as a repeating ground. It is an allover lattice with no centre, so it tiles behind content without pulling the eye to a focal point.

Navratna has a real centre, which makes it the one to wrap क in.

Rendering note. Cells are merged into horizontal runs before drawing — a 44-grid is 1,936 cells, and emitting one rect each would be absurd. Each plate reports what it actually drew.

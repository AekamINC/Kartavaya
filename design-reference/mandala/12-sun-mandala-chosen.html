Sun mandala क — bigger petals, bigger letter

Built from Meditation Mandala 8972474, then opened up. The petals were the smallest thing in a drawing that is meant to be a flower — the rays dominated it completely — so the rosette has doubled, a full course of twenty petals now spans the outer band with the rays weaving between them, and क has gone from 26px to 46px with an eye wide enough to hold it.

Everything else is the reference: one colour, one hairline, long curved rays that sweep rather than spoke, and the whole figure drawing itself on stroke by stroke before undrawing. That draw-on is a Lottie trim path; in CSS it is stroke-dashoffset — the same mechanism, so this is the motion itself rather than an impression of it.

Three timings, one figure

Same drawing throughout. Only the pace and the direction of the underlying turn change.

How it is built

Petals inside and out. Ten large lobes with ten smaller between them at the centre, and twenty full petals spanning the outer band — each with a bead in its throat. A first attempt put a small 24-unit fringe at the rim, which read as an edging rather than as structure: the outside still belonged to the rays. These are the outside.

The rays weave between them. Offset half a step, so a ray rises out of every gap. Laid over the petals they merely crossed them; in the gaps the two courses interlock and the outer band reads as one piece of drawing. Verified at dead centre — 9 degrees into an 18-degree step.

The eye opens to fit the letter. Its radius went from 11 to 32 — क is sized first and the ornament makes room, rather than the letter being shrunk to fit what was already there. At the plate size that is 65px of clear diameter for a 46px letter.

The rays curve. Each is sampled along a spiral — radius and angle advance together — so it sweeps as it extends, with the bend easing tighter toward the tip. Straight spokes read as a gear; swept ones read as a sun.

Every stroke carries its own length. A ray and a rosette petal differ by nearly six times. One shared dasharray would draw them at wildly different rates and the figure would assemble raggedly, so each element measures itself.

Fine mandala सूक्ष्म — four traditions, drawn properly

The first set was a ring of petals. These are built the way the work is actually made: petals nesting in the gaps of the ring beneath them, an echo line inside every petal, bindu at the tips, scalloped bands between the courses, and a toothed rim. Counts are the traditional ones — 8, 16, 32, 64. Each plate says how many elements it draws.

The plates

All four still centre on क, and all four are generated from numbers rather than drawn by hand — petal counts, radii, ring offsets. Watch them build.

What changed from the first set

Offset nesting. Every ring is rotated half a step against the one below it, so petals sit in the gaps rather than stacking on top. This single rule is most of the difference between a flower icon and a lotus.

Echo lines. Each petal carries a second outline inset within it. It doubles the line count at no extra structure and is what gives hand-drawn work its depth.

Weight hierarchy. Structural circles at 1.1, ornament at 0.55, bindu dots smaller still. A drawing where every line is the same weight reads as clip art.

Rims that finish. Scalloped bands and fine teeth close each composition. Ornament that just stops at its outermost ring always looks unfinished.

Ten fine-line mandalas क

The reference set is the opposite of what I had been building. Every one of those is a single hairline weight in a single solid colour — no weight hierarchy, no tonal layers, no opacity fading. I had been treating weight hierarchy as a virtue, and it is exactly what made mine read as designed graphics rather than as drawn line art. The other difference is count: those outer rings carry forty to eighty tiny elements, not sixteen.

So: one weight, one colour, high counts, circular rim. क in the middle of each.

The ten

Alternate courses turn opposite ways and nothing fades out, so the figure is complete at every instant — which is what makes these work as loaders rather than as decorations that occasionally disappear.

The three rules I got wrong before

One stroke weight. Every line in the reference is the same hairline. I had structural circles at 1.4 and lace at 0.42, which is textbook advice and wrong here — uniform weight is what makes a mandala read as drawn with one pen.

One colour, full strength. No opacity ramp. I had been fading outer courses to 0.32, which greys the drawing and kills the crispness.

Counts of 40–80. Fine repetition IS the texture. At sixteen you get a flower; at sixty you get lace.

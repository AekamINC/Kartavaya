The paperwork that unblocks the sale

Four public documents and one internal runbook, written to the DPDP Act and shipped as real routes. None of it is a certificate — no certificate for DPDP exists. What it is: the answer to every question an Indian firm's counsel asks before signing, in a form they can read without asking us.

The thing worth knowing first

Nobody certifies anyone as "DPDP compliant." The Act creates obligations and a regulator, not an accreditation scheme. The certificates buyers actually recognise are SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001, and both are months and lakhs away.

What closes deals in the meantime is documentation: a firm's counsel wants a privacy policy, a DPA they can sign, a named sub-processor list, and a security page. Those are free. This proposal is those four, plus the internal process that makes their promises true.

Four decisions worth arguing with

1. One source of truth, not four documents

Every checkable fact lives in frontend/src/pages/legal/legalFacts.js and is rendered from there. The retention period in the DPA and the retention period in the policy are the same constant, so they cannot drift.

This matters more here than in most code. The audience is accounting firms — people who cross-check documents for a living. A DPA promising 90-day deletion beside a policy promising 30 is not a typo to them; it is evidence that nobody is minding the process.

2. Missing facts shout instead of pretending

Facts only you can supply are TKTK(...) and render as an amber MISSING — what is owed marker. Eight remain. While any remain, every page carries a banner saying it is a draft and not a statement of Aekam Inc's position.

The failure this prevents is specific: a plausible-looking placeholder ("123 Business Park, Andheri East") surviving into production, where it stops being a placeholder and becomes a false statement on a legal document. A box that says MISSING cannot be mistaken for content.

3. The sub-processor list was derived, not remembered

The fourteen entries came from grepping outbound hosts in backend/services/ and backend/routers/, not from recalling what we signed up for. A list assembled from memory is wrong within a quarter and nobody notices until a diligence questionnaire catches it — at which point the entire document set loses its credibility at the worst possible moment.

They are grouped by when the vendor comes into play rather than alphabetically, because the question a reader arrives with is "who sees my data if I do nothing?", and an alphabetical list only answers it after every row has been read.

Always  6 vendors
Supabase, Railway, Vercel, Cloudflare R2, Resend, Sentry. No configuration avoids them.
Feature-dependent  4
Expo, Gemini, Serper, Apify. Off until an administrator switches the module on; switching off stops the transmission.
Your own connections  4 groups
Meta, the social platforms, Google Business Profile, IndiaMART and JustDial. Reached with credentials the firm supplies.

4. The security page names its own gaps

Section 8 of /security is headed What we do not have yet and lists four things: no SOC 2, no ISO 27001, no published penetration test, no MFA, no Indian data residency.

This follows the rule the landing page's Trust section already set — only claims that are true and checkable. A security page that lists strengths and omits gaps gets read by a CA firm as a page that is hiding something, and the omission surfaces during diligence rather than during the sale. Naming them costs one paragraph and buys the other seven sections their credibility.

The residency answer stays honest. The database is in Singapore and the pages say so, in three places. Indian law does not currently require general personal data to stay in India, and no country has been notified as restricted under s.16 — so the honest answer is also a sufficient one. It only becomes a problem if a customer discovers it after we implied otherwise.

And one that is not a blocker but is not optional either

None of this has been reviewed by counsel. It is a complete draft written to the Act — the right shape, the right clauses, defensible reasoning — but a lawyer has not read it. Get an Indian data protection lawyer over the DPA and the privacy policy before the banner comes down. That is a few hours of billing against a document that will be attached to every contract you sign for the next three years.

The certificates, and when to buy them

Documentation unblocks the SMB sale. Certificates unblock the enterprise one, and they are worth buying in this order and not the reverse.

StandardWho asksWhenRough cost
ISO 27001:2022 Indian enterprise, EU, most RFPs First. The SPDI Rules name it as the "reasonable security practices" safe harbour under IT Act s.43A — so it caps statutory liability, which SOC 2 does not. ₹4–10L yr 1
SOC 2 Type II US buyers, VC diligence When a US customer or investor demands it. Type II needs a 3–12 month observation window, so start the tooling well before the audit. $15–40K/yr
ISO 27701 Privacy-conscious enterprise Bolt-on once 27001 exists; cheap by comparison +₹2–3L
GDPR EU customers No certificate exists. A DPA, SCCs and a records-of-processing document — legal fees only —
Not needed: PCI DSS (no gateway — an invoice is marked paid only from bank reconciliation, so cardholder data never touches us), HIPAA, FedRAMP.

The trap is buying SOC 2 first because it sounds more prestigious. To an Indian CA firm it means very little, and it does nothing for s.43A liability.

Two things the gate suite caught, and what they were right about

Worth recording, because both are cases where the ratchets earned their keep on a change that had nothing to do with what they were built to guard.

  • check-orphan-selectors found .lgl__h3 — a rule written and then never used. Deleted rather than baselined. CSS that ships without its markup is the first millimetre of the drift these checks exist to stop.
  • check-table-rows flagged the sub-processor table for not sitting on --row-h. It is now in EXEMPT with a reason: every cell is a wrapping sentence, so a fixed 66px would either clip the description or hollow out the short rows. The token exists to give a comfortable hit target on a row you act on, and nothing in this table is clickable. It also has to print — a forced row height paginates a long description across a page break.

What happens next

  1. Create and staff the two mailboxes. Nothing else can go live first.
  2. Fill the eight facts in legalFacts.js. The banner disappears on its own when the last one lands — LEGAL_READY is computed, not set.
  3. Lawyer reads the DPA and the privacy policy.
  4. Ship. Add the four links to the sales deck and the RFP response boilerplate.
  5. Fix the CERT-In gap: 180 days of logs with an India-resident copy. This is the one live legal exposure the documents describe but the infrastructure does not yet meet, and it is independent of everything above.
  6. Start ISO 27001 when there are two or three paying enterprise customers.

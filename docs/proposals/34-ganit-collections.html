How the service is known

Two independent signals, and they are not equally strong.

Scan eventFact. The customer opened pay.kartavaya.com/i/…?via=phonepe. We logged the service they chose, the device, OS, browser, coarse city from IP, and whether the app actually opened. This proves intent, not payment — they may have closed the app without paying.
Payer handleStrong inference. UPI credits arrive with the payer's VPA in the bank narration, and the handle names the app: @ybl is PhonePe, @paytm is Paytm, @ok… is Google Pay, @upi is BHIM. This is what the table above is attributed from, and it works even when the customer never touched our page.
Both agreeScan says PhonePe, handle says @ybl, amount and timing match — attributed with confidence and no badge shown.
They disagreeScanned via Google Pay, paid from @ybl. The handle wins — it came from the bank, the scan is only intent. The row is flagged so you can see it happened.
NeitherCash, cheque, NEFT, or a bank-app scan of the standard code. Shown as Bank transfer / Unattributed. Never guessed.

What this cannot do — read before approving

  • UPI never tells us which app paid. There is no field in the settlement that names PhonePe or Google Pay. Every service label on this screen is inferred from the payer's handle or from a scan. It will be right in the large majority of cases and it will sometimes be wrong.
  • It depends on the bank narration carrying the payer VPA. Some banks truncate it. Where it is missing, the payment lands as Unattributed — which is why that slice exists in the chart above rather than being quietly folded into the others.
  • The location is city-level from IP, and IP is wrong often enough to matter. A customer on a corporate VPN in Mumbai paying for a Surat delivery will read as Mumbai. It is useful as a pattern, never as evidence.
  • These are your clients' customers, not your users. IP, device and location are personal data under the DPDP Act. Collecting them needs a stated purpose, a retention limit, and a line in the privacy notice. My recommendation: keep the full IP for 30 days for fraud and duplicate-payment checks, then truncate to the city and discard the rest. That is what the masked 49.36.x.x in the feed is showing.
  • Nothing here proves payment on its own. The bank statement remains the source of truth. This screen explains how money arrived; it never decides whether it did.

What it takes to build

  • ganit_payments gains payer_vpa, service and attribution (observed / inferred / none). The service belongs to the payment, not the invoice — row three above is why.
  • A new ganit_pay_scans table: token, service chosen, device, OS, browser, truncated IP, city, outcome, timestamp.
  • The bank reconciliation already parses narration — the payer handle is extracted there, so attribution rides on work that already exists rather than a new integration.
  • The service marks shown are wordmarks. Real PhonePe, GPay and Paytm logos need their official brand kits before this ships to clients.

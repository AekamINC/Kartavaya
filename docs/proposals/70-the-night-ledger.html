The correction that reorders everything

One workflow said every skill template is seeded {"type":"cron","interval_minutes":N}. The other said every row is NULL. Those argue for opposite decisions — fix the scheduler versus fill in the column — so I read the live table.

All nineteen are NULL. The /cron/skills query filters on trigger_config->>'type' = 'cron' and therefore selects zero templates. The first agent was reading the migration file, not the database.

Which means: every one of the 104 skill runs was a human pressing a button. Nothing has ever fired on its own. The marketplace is not an automation product that is failing — it is a set of buttons nobody visits.

The shape of the failure

104 runs in the product's life. 52 failed, and every single one says the same four words: Insufficient credits. 38 more sit at running and always will. 14 completed.

And the mechanism, verified in the handlers

The failures are not really about credits either. Four of the nineteen skills cannot complete a run at all, because their handlers declare a parameter with no default and nothing supplies it:

check_gstr1_readiness(pool, org_id, period) · brief_gstr3b_liability(pool, org_id, period) · get_my_desk(… user_id) · get_account_brief(… contact_id)

Both GST skills — the two most valuable things in the catalogue — are in that list. The fix is already written two directories away, in payroll_readiness.py:57: month = month or utc_now().strftime("%Y-%m"). Four one-line defaults turn four dead skills into working ones, with no new SQL, no new card, and no marketplace surface at all.

Ganit — finance & GST · the eight that pay for the tier
01

The 180-Day Reversal

Before every GSTR-3B: every vendor bill past 180 days and still unpaid, with the tax you must reverse, grouped by vendor.

FreeMonthly · 15th–19thRule 37

Best-evidenced idea in the list — the query already runs and returns 42 rows on the seeded org. Rule 37 reverses ITC where the supplier is unpaid 180 days from the invoice date, and it is re-availed on payment with no time limit, so the output must name what comes back, not just what goes out.

Ship it worded as “credit at risk of reversal”, never “the exact tax you must reverse”. There is no ITC-availed flag in the product, it cannot see RCM supplies, and it says nothing about s.50 interest. The first CA who runs it will try to tie the number out.

The dependency the catalogue got wrong

It says the gating migration is the client registration profile. It is not. It is a dated statute table — effective_from/effective_to against form number, section number, threshold, rate, due day and state.

Twelve-plus entries print a form, a section, a threshold or a date. The forms were renumbered on 1 April 2026 (16→130, 16A→131, 24Q→138, 26Q→140, 27Q→144, 27EQ→143) and the sections were renumbered with them (206AA→s.397(2), 43B(h)→s.37(2)(g)). The catalogue corrected the form numbers and then printed the old section numbers throughout. Until that table exists, every statutory skill hardcodes law into Python and goes stale silently — the exact failure the research identified and then did not design against.

Why Dristi cannot simply absorb it

A Dristi metric request carries exactly four things — org_id, window, bucket, group_by (registry.py:34-40). There is no invoice filter, no employee filter, no project filter, and no “give me the rows” mode. All 107 metrics return a number or a small series.

So Dristi and /reports are today the same kind of thing: a summary you look at. Neither can produce what a firm needs at filing time, which is a list — these 47 invoices, this employee-by-day grid, these 12 vendors and what is owed to each. What absorbs /reports is not the metric registry; it is the rendering and delivery spine.

The instruction I would strike as written

The plan's step 9 says “implement the drill field that 74 metrics already declare”. Unqualified, that is an instruction to build navigation from an analytics tile into payslips and the employee register — thirteen drills point into personal registers across manav.py and vetana.py.

Pahchan declares zero drills and a test enforces it: “a drill would grant through analytics what the module itself refuses.” Every drill needs an entitlement check at the destination, not at the tile. And drill is not unread — pulse.py:669 emits it and that test consumes it as a DPDP invariant.

Who actually reads a report

Every agent asked what a report is. None asked who reads one, or on what. mobile/src/nav/destinations.ts lists nineteen destinations and there is no Reports; Dristi is there and says so in words — report builders and exports “need width and stay on desktop”. So a catalogue of fifty reports is built for a surface the owner of an Indian firm is not on.

  • The owner wants one number, on a phone, unprompted. Served only by a delivered report.
  • The accountant wants a worklist — GSTR-1 defects, unmatched bank lines — and only at filing time. The only reader a searchable catalogue serves.
  • The client wants one document on the firm's letterhead, by email.

So reverse the plan's order. It puts search at step 2 and delivery at step 8. Timing beats findability: search reaches someone already looking; a due-date arrival reaches someone who is not. A statutory report that lands on its due date, on a phone, is worth more than forty reports behind a search box.

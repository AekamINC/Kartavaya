The finding that reframes the inbox

Nearly half the task table was written by test accounts. 351 of 736 tasks were created by a Playwright or QA login. Two of those logins hold real user_roles in Unicode Group, a live organisation, and five hold active memberships in its projects. One of them is an admin of “Keval To Do” and authored 13 of its 39 tasks.

This is why the email you received looked like a leak and was not one: the test account was a genuine member, so every fan-out correctly included it. The bug was that the fan-out went to everyone; the test accounts simply made it visible.

The consequence for this scope: several inbox items describe symptoms whose cause is polluted data, not broken code. Cleaning first will retire some of the list without a line being written — and will stop me diagnosing ghosts.

Every glass animation, one at a time

Eight surfaces, each on its own. Everything here is the CSS that shipped in 4520090d and ece21d78 — same tokens, same durations, same easings.

Hover things. Nothing here needs a login.
1The sidebar lozenge one element, moved — so it slides

Click between modules. The highlight is a single element positioned by offsetTop, so travelling from one to the next is movement — two per-item highlights could only cross-fade, which reads as a flicker. Hover a row it is not on to see the lighter hover glass underneath it.

2The rail waking on approach blur and saturation lift while the pointer is inside

At rest the rail is calm; the material shows itself when you reach for it. This is the one piece from proposal 45 not yet in the build — it is two lines and I have held it back until you have seen it here.

3The button sheen one pass of light, on hover

A pseudo-element, not a background animation — all seven variants own their own background and a keyframe on it would fight every one. On the light-ground variants the sheen is primary-tinted, because white on cream is invisible. Off on :disabled and off under reduced-motion.

4Popover and menu dmPop · 140ms · spring

This is where the Windows override cost the most: a popover has a whole table behind it, so the blur had plenty to work on and was switched off anyway.

5Modal over a scrim dmScrim + dmModal · 220ms

The scrim carries its own 2px blur, so the page behind softens as the panel arrives rather than only darkening.

6Drawer and bottom sheet dmDrawer · dmSheet · 220ms

The sheet is what every picker becomes below 768px — the date picker included, which is why it is glass on a phone and a popover on a desktop.

7Toast and bulk bar dmToast · dmBulk · 220ms

Both rise rather than fade — they arrive from the edge they belong to, which is what tells you they are transient rather than part of the page.

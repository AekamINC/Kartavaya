What the competition actually does

Eight procurement products and five Indian HR/payroll platforms, read from their own documentation, help centres, review sites and community threads. Then the four gaps none of them fill — and what that means for the Finance and People suite we already have.

Proposal 78 · researched 2026-08-22 · every claim sourced and linked at the foot · nothing here is recalled from memory

The four findings, before the detail

  1. Nobody checks a budget before the PO. This is NetSuite's own documented gap, and it is why a whole category of "procurement layer in front of the ERP" exists at all.
  2. Amending a confirmed PO is broken almost everywhere. Odoo makes you cancel and recreate. It is the single most-asked question at every vendor.
  3. Payroll is an island in most Indian HR suites. The ones where it is not are the ones people keep. We already have one employee record.
  4. GSTR-2B reconciliation is the CA firm's real daily pain, and it is downstream of the purchase register — which the PO module creates. One measured case: vendor response rates went from 30% to 70% once follow-up was systematic.

What this tells us

The cheapest dedicated tool is $310/month and the mid one is $999. An Indian practice with fifteen people is not buying that, and none of these three knows what a GSTIN is. The gap is not "a better Precoro" — it is a competent PO module inside the system the firm already pays for.

And note the shape of the complaints: too many steps for a small purchase, reporting too shallow, customisation too rigid. Those are arguments for approval being optional and rule-matched — exactly the design already decided in proposal 77 — rather than a mandatory gate.

Three things worth stealing outright

  • Tally's Reorder Status — closing stock, pending purchase orders, pending sales orders, net available, shortfall, in one report. We have stock and we will have POs; this is nearly free once both exist.
  • Odoo's vendor pricelists — price by quantity break with validity dates, plus lead time and vendor priority per product. It is what makes "which supplier, at what price" answerable instead of remembered.
  • Odoo's backorder — validating a partial receipt automatically creates the remainder as a backorder rather than leaving a person to track it.

Part two · The FAQ, which is the real spec

Help-centre questions are the best public evidence of what users actually hit. These recur at every vendor:

The questionThe market's answerWhat we should do
Can I edit an approved PO? Precoro: yes, via revise — by the initiator or an approver, even mid-matching. Odoo: no, cancel and recreate. Revise, always. A version with a diff and an approver.
Does editing re-trigger approval? Reverting to pending re-routes to approvers. A small edit inside the existing authorisation flows through; a material rise in value, scope or term needs fresh approval down the same path. Exactly this, with "material" as a setting.
What survives a revision? Each version shows what changed and who approved it; the original is not overwritten. A cancelled-and-revised PO carries the original's history log. Same. The history is the audit trail.
Half arrived — now what? Partial receipts validated as they come; a bill matched against a partial receipt is flagged partial match and held for review; AP pays the delivered portion and releases the balance when the second receipt is logged. Same, plus the automatic backorder.
How do I prove a tool handles this? Vendors themselves recommend demoing with a real purchase request, a revised PO, a partial delivery, an invoice mismatch and a document exception. That is our e2e test list. Five scenarios, named by the industry, that any credible PO module must survive.

The sentence that matters most in this entire document

"Some platforms run payroll on the same employee record that powers attendance and core HR, and others treat payroll as an island that needs to be fed reconciled data every cycle."

That is the structural divide in the Indian HR market, and we are already on the right side of it. manav_employees is the single record; attendance, leave, salary structures, payslips, commission and bonus all hang off it. Nothing has to be reconciled between modules each cycle because nothing is separate.

Zoho — the most likely incumbent at a firm our size — is explicitly on the wrong side: People and Payroll are different subscriptions. Every month, somebody moves data between them.

Part four · The four gaps, and what to build

Gap 1 · Budget checked before the PO

NetSuite cannot do it natively. It is the reason the procurement-layer category exists. We can, because the PO and the ledger are in one database — no integration, no nightly sync. Committed spend against a limit, refused at the point of raising, is a feature a ₹1,000/month product cannot offer without buying a second one.

Caveat carried over from proposal 77: budgets keyed on department need departments to be real records. manav_employees.department is free text and blank on 11 of 98.

Gap 2 · Amend without cancelling

The most-asked question in the market, and Odoo — the most-deployed open ERP — answers it with "cancel and recreate". Revisions with a diff, a reason and a re-approval rule is a small amount of code and a large amount of daylight.

Gap 3 · GSTR-2B reconciliation, fed by the purchase register

This is the CA firm's actual daily pain, and it sits directly downstream of the PO module. A bill must exist in both the purchase register and GSTR-2B or the ITC is delayed or denied. Tools that automate the match cut the cycle from days to hours; one documented case moved vendor response rates from 30% to 70% purely by making follow-up systematic.

We already have the follow-up machinery. The skills shelf names a vendor, gives a phone number and an email, and links to the record — that work shipped this week. A "chase the vendors whose invoices are missing from 2B" skill is a small addition to something that already exists, and it is worth more to a CA firm than the PO module itself.

Gap 4 · The vendor channel is WhatsApp, not email

The finding is blunt: the default in basic PO tools is "send PO as email PDF", and where a supplier actually works over another channel, the tool's Sent status is weaker than it looks — the order moved somewhere the tool cannot see. Large suppliers use portals, WhatsApp or EDI.

In India the answer is WhatsApp, and we have the Cloud API wired. A PO sent over WhatsApp with a delivery receipt is something Precoro cannot do at $999 a month.

Where we are genuinely behind

Honesty is worth more here than a clean scorecard.

  • Compliance turnaround. greytHR ships a statutory change 1–2 weeks after the circular. That is a decades-old operational muscle, not a feature, and we have nothing like it. Our statute calendar is dated and read as_of — the right architecture — but the process that keeps it current is a person, and right now that person is you.
  • Professional Tax coverage. Keka names seven states explicitly; greytHR claims the widest coverage of non-standard states and UTs. Ours needs auditing against that bar rather than assumed equal.
  • Payroll edge cases. Arrears, shift differentials, mid-cycle joiners and leavers, and unpaid-leave interactions are named strengths at Keka. They are where payroll actually breaks, and they deserve a test sweep before we claim parity.
  • Form 16 / 24Q. All four competitors generate them. We have pack_form130_annexure, which is explicit that it is a working paper and not the certificate — correct, and still a gap a buyer will ask about.

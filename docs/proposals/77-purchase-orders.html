Purchase orders

Decisions already taken

  • Approval is optional, and configurable — not a fixed step. Who approves what kind of PO, whether two approvers are needed, thresholds, and the PO prefix all live in PO module settings.

What customers ask for, over and over

These are the questions the help centres of every PO product answer, which is the best available evidence of what people actually hit. Each one is a design requirement.

"Can I edit a PO after it has been approved?"

The single most-asked question, at every vendor. The answer has to be yes — and the answer has to be a revision, not an overwrite. Each version shows what changed and who approved it; the original is never destroyed.

"Does editing it send it back for approval?"

Depends on the change, and the rule is well established: a small edit within the existing authorisation flows through; a change that materially raises the value, adds scope, or extends the term needs fresh approval — down the same path it would have taken originally. We implement exactly that, and the threshold for "material" is a setting.

"Half the order arrived. Now what?"

Partial receipt, and it is the feature that separates real PO systems from document generators. Covered in full below.

"The vendor billed us for more than they delivered."

Three-way match: PO against receipt against bill, with discrepancies flagged rather than silently accepted. This is the exception worth actual money.

"Can I close a PO that will never be fully delivered?"

Close short — with a reason. Without it, every partially-fulfilled order sits open for ever and the committed-spend figure is permanently wrong.

"How much have we committed this quarter?"

Budget tracking with limits and alerts as spend approaches them. Not every tool has it; the ones that do are the ones finance teams keep.

"Can the vendor see it without me emailing a PDF?"

The honest finding here is a warning, not a feature: "send PO as email PDF" covers only part of a supplier mix, and a tool that only emails shows a Sent status that is weaker than it looks — the order may actually have moved over WhatsApp. Since we already have WhatsApp, this is ours to win.

What India adds that no foreign product handles

RuleWhat it means for a PO
GST place of supply Inter-state is IGST, intra-state splits CGST+SGST. The PO must decide this the same way the invoice does, from the vendor's state — or the PO total and the bill total differ and the three-way match fails on tax alone.
Section 194Q TDS A buyer with turnover over ₹10 crore deducts 0.1% on purchases from a resident seller past ₹50 lakh in a year. It bites at payment or credit, whichever is earlier — and advances count. A PO is where a firm first sees it coming, so the module should warn when a vendor is approaching the threshold rather than letting the bill be the first anyone hears of it.
194Q on the gross Turnover for the ₹10cr test excludes GST; the TDS itself is computed on the purchase value including GST. Two different bases, and getting them the wrong way round is a real filing error.
MSME payment clock Already in this product, already reading acceptance_date. The PO receipt is what should set it.
HSN on every line The catalogue already carries it. The PO must too, or the bill cannot be matched line to line.
None of Precoro, Procurify or Coupa know what 194Q is. This is the part a CA firm cannot get from a foreign tool at any price.

The hard problem: three quantities, not one

An ordinary document has a quantity. A purchase-order line has three, and they are routinely all different:

QuantityMeansSet when
orderedwhat we asked forthe PO is issued
receivedwhat turned upeach delivery
billedwhat the vendor charged foreach bill

The gaps between them are the entire module. ordered > received is a late supplier. received > billed is an accrual — goods received not invoiced, which a CA needs at period end. billed > received is a vendor charging for goods that never came, which is the one worth real money.

A design storing one quantity per line cannot express any of those states, and no reporting on top recovers them. Which is why receipts are their own table — a counter that gets overwritten loses the arrival history, and "when did this arrive?" is exactly what the MSME clock and any dispute both turn on.

Settings — the part you asked for

All of it lives in Finance → Purchase orders → Settings, per org. Nothing here is mandatory; an org that configures none of it gets a PO module that numbers documents and never asks anyone for permission.

SettingShapeDefault
Prefix Letters only, 2–8 characters — same rule and same storage as the invoice prefixes shipped this week (settings.doc_prefixes). PO
Approval required Off / On. Off means the whole approval concept never appears. Off
Approval rules An ordered list. Each rule matches on amount ≥ X, department, and/or category, and names who approves and how many must approve. First match wins; no match means no approval needed. empty
Two-approver rules A property of the rule, not a separate feature: approvers_required: 2. Sequential or either-of is a per-rule choice. 1
Re-approval threshold A revision that raises the total by more than X% or ₹Y goes back for approval. Directly from the FAQ finding above. 10% or ₹10,000
Self-approval May a person approve a PO they raised? Not allowed
Budget limits Optional per department per period, with an alert threshold. off
Receiving Whether over-receipt is allowed, and by what tolerance. refuse over-receipt
Close-short reasons The firm's own list, so the reason is a value and not free text nobody reports on. a starter list

Why "first match wins" and not a chain

Every ERP eventually grows multi-level chains, and every one of them becomes impossible for the customer to reason about. An ordered list where the first matching rule decides is something a person can read aloud — "anything over two lakh in Audit needs both partners" — and it covers the amount/department/category axes that the ERPs themselves reduce to. If a firm genuinely needs sequential escalation, approvers_required: 2 with a sequential flag gives it without a graph.

Invoice lines are jsonb because nothing queries inside them; PO lines are queried constantly — "what is on order for this product" is the second question anyone asks.

ganit_po_revisions po_id, revision, changed_by, changed_at, diff (jsonb), reason, re_approved The answer to the most-asked question. The original is never destroyed; each version records what changed and who approved it.
ganit_po_approvals po_id, revision, approver_id, decided_at, decision, note One row per approver per revision, so a two-approver rule is expressible and a rejection keeps its reason.
Plus a nullable po_id on ganit_vendor_bills. A bill WITHOUT a PO stays legal — most firms raise POs for some spend and not all, and refusing an un-ordered bill would stop them recording real invoices.

What the module answers

  • Committed spend — issued, not yet received. Does not exist today in any form.
  • Goods received, not invoiced — the period-end accrual, currently assembled by hand or not at all.
  • Billed but never received — the exception worth money.
  • Late suppliers — expected date passed with quantity outstanding. A skill, and it fits the shelf's shape exactly: vendor name, phone number, days late.
  • 194Q approaching — a vendor nearing ₹50 lakh for the year, warned at PO time rather than at bill time.
  • Budget vs committed, per department, where limits are set.

Still excluded — and why

ExcludedReason
Requisitions (a request to raise a PO) A second document in front of the first, valuable only where the asker and the buyer are different people. Neither live org is that shape. Adding it later costs nothing because it hangs off the PO rather than changing it.
Blanket / call-off POs Genuinely a different object — a standing agreement drawn down over time. Guessing at it now shapes the schema wrongly; it is additive later.
Automatic three-way-match approval The data for the match is built. Automatically approving a bill because it matches is a decision to make after somebody has watched the match be right for a few months. The exceptions are surfaced from day one.
Landed cost apportionment Freight and duty split across lines. Real, and squarely an inventory feature — it belongs with Vikray stock.
EDI / supplier portals Enterprise supplier integration. We have WhatsApp, which is the channel these suppliers actually use.

One thing I would still push back on

Budget limits per department assume departments hold budgets. In this product manav_employees.department is a free-text column, not a modelled entity with an owner — and 11 of 98 employees have it blank. Budgets keyed on a string nobody governs will quietly stop matching. I would build the setting and leave it off by default, and flag that making it dependable needs departments to become real records first.

Purchase orders

A module, not a field. What it has to record, what the database already gives us, the one hard problem inside it, and what I would deliberately leave out of the first version.

Proposal 77 · branch staging · every count below measured read-only against the live database on 2026-08-22

What a purchase order is, in one sentence

A commitment to spend money that has not been spent yet — and that is the whole reason it needs its own object. Every other financial record in this product describes something that has already happened. A vendor bill is a debt that exists. A payment is money that has moved. A purchase order is the only record of money a firm has promised and not yet owed.

Without it, the question "what have we committed to this quarter?" has no answer here, and the first time a commitment appears in the books is when the vendor's bill arrives — by which point it is too late to disagree with it.

So this is not a green field. The counterparty, the catalogue and the destination all exist and are populated. What is missing is the object in between — and the fact that ganit_vendor_bills already carries line_items as jsonb, the same shape invoices use, means a PO can be built to pour into it rather than beside it.

One thing to decide before any code

ganit_vendor_bills.acceptance_date already exists and is read by the MSME payment-clock check. If a PO introduces its own idea of "received on", the two will disagree the first time somebody uses both — and the MSME clock is a statutory deadline, not a nicety. The PO must write that column, not shadow it.

Editable. No number yet — a serial spent on a draft is a gap in the series.

Optional, per org. Below a threshold it can skip straight to issued.
03
Issued
Numbered, sent to the vendor, no longer freely editable.
04
Part received
Some quantity has arrived. This is the state that makes it hard.
05
Closed
Fully received and billed, or closed short on purpose.

Four of those five are ordinary. Part received is where every purchase-order system earns or loses its keep, and it is worth being explicit about why.

The hard problem: partial receipt

Three quantities per line, not one

An ordinary document has a quantity. A purchase-order line has three, and they are routinely all different:

QuantityMeansSet when
orderedwhat we asked forthe PO is issued
receivedwhat actually turned upeach delivery
billedwhat the vendor has charged foreach bill
The gaps between them are the entire value of the module. ordered > received is a supplier who is late. received > billed is a bill still to come — an accrual. billed > received is a vendor charging for goods that never arrived, which is the one a firm most wants caught.

A design that stores one quantity per line cannot express any of those three states, and no amount of reporting on top can recover them.

Which means receipts are their own table

A delivery is an event with a date, a person and a quantity, and there can be several against one line. Storing received_qty on the line and overwriting it loses the history — and "when did this arrive?" is exactly the question the MSME clock and any dispute both turn on. So: ganit_po_receipts, one row per delivery per line.

Schema

TableHoldsNotes that matter
ganit_purchase_orders org, vendor, po_number, po_date, expected_date, status, currency, totals, terms, delivery address, created_by, approved_by, approved_at Numbered from next_doc_number — which needs a new entry in _ALLOWED_DOC_TABLES, currently four tables. Prefix PO, overridable per org through the doc_prefixes setting shipped this week.
ganit_po_lines po_id, product_id (nullable), description, hsn, qty_ordered, unit, rate, gst_rate, line_total A real table, not jsonb. Invoices keep lines as jsonb because nothing ever queries inside them; PO lines are queried constantly — "what is on order for this product" is the second question anyone asks.
ganit_po_receipts po_line_id, qty, received_on, received_by, note One row per delivery. Never overwritten, so the arrival history survives. Feeds ganit_vendor_bills.acceptance_date rather than competing with it.
No fourth table for the bill link: ganit_vendor_bills gains a nullable po_id. A bill without a PO stays legal — most firms will raise POs for some spend and not all, and a design that refuses an un-ordered bill would stop them recording real invoices.

What the module answers

  • Committed spend — issued POs not yet fully received. The number that does not exist today at all.
  • Goods received, not invoiced — the accrual a CA firm needs at period end, currently assembled by hand or not at all.
  • Billed but never received — the exception worth real money.
  • Late suppliers — expected_date passed with quantity outstanding. This is a skill, and it fits the shelf's existing shape exactly: name the vendor, the phone number, the days late.

What I would leave out of version one

Left outWhy
Multi-level approval chains One approver, one threshold. Chains are a settings screen and a state machine for a problem no user of this product has reported having.
Requisitions (a request to raise a PO) A second document in front of the first, valuable only in firms large enough to separate the asker from the buyer. Neither live org is.
Blanket / open-ended POs Call-offs against a standing order are a genuinely different object. Adding it later costs nothing; guessing at it now shapes the schema wrongly.
Three-way match automation The data for it is what this proposal builds. Automatically approving a bill because it matches is a decision to make after somebody has watched the match be right for a few months.
Landed cost apportionment Freight and duty split across lines. Real, and squarely an inventory feature — it belongs with Vikray stock, not here.

The decision I need from you

Is approval in or out of version one? It is the difference between a document a buyer raises and sends, and a document that waits for somebody. Everything else above I would build the same way either way — but approval changes the state machine, adds a screen, and is the one part that is awkward to add afterwards because issued POs would already exist without ever having been approved.

My recommendation: include a single optional approver with a threshold, off by default. A firm that does not want it never sees it, and one that does is not asking for a migration six weeks later.

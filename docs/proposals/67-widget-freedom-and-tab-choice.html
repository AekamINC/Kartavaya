The board you arrange

Two things, both live on this page: analytics widgets you drag and resize the way Monday or SharePoint lets you, and module tabs you reorder yourself — including which tab a module opens on. Everything here is the real interaction contract; saying yes turns this into product code.

Demo 1 · Drag & resize widgets

Press Customise, then drag a card by its ⠿ grip to move it — the others make room and the board packs itself upward. Drag the corner handle to resize and watch the data grow with the card: a wider trend gains months, a taller table gains rows, a wide table gains its invoice-number column, a grown KPI earns a 12-month sparkline. Everything snaps to a 12-column grid. Keyboard: focus a grip, Enter to pick up, arrows to move, Enter to drop, Esc to cancel. On a resize handle the arrows change the size directly.

Finance board वित्त Viewing — arrangement is per person, per view.

On a phone the board reads top-to-bottom in your saved order — arranging happens on a bigger screen.

Ships as: the arrangement saves into the same saved-view record the analytics tab already keeps per person (personal → org default → preset → derived), so your board follows you to any device. Org admins get a “Save for everyone” door. Phones stack the same order, top-to-bottom.

Demo 2 · Tabs you choose

The Ganit strip below is live. Open More → Customise tabs… to reorder what sits on the strip, and star the tab this module should open on. Nothing is ever hidden — what doesn’t fit waits in More.

Ships as: saved per person, per module on the server (an org-default row underneath, admin-settable), so it follows you across web and the phone app. New tabs we ship land in More — they never steal a slot you arranged. Deep links still win over the starred default.

  • Fits the house rules. Fluid, left-aligned, k-* tokens; no UUID ever rendered; keyboard paths for every pointer gesture; reduced-motion honoured.
  • No new dependency. The grid engine is ~150 lines of pointer math + collision packing — the same discipline as this page — not react-grid-layout.
  • Density follows size. The renderer chooses how much a card shows from its cell size — a 4-column trend shows a quarter, a 12-column one the year; a taller table renders more rows and says how many wait below. No extra requests: the data is already there, the card decides what fits.
  • Old views keep working. Existing saved views (width 1–3, no positions) upgrade on first open: widths scale ×4, positions pack in reading order, nothing asks the user.
  • Reports unaffected. The emailed/PDF module report keeps its print layout; it reads the same widget list and ignores screen geometry.

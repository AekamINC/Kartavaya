Procurement as its own module क्रय

Proposal 85 · rescope · 23 August 2026 · supersedes proposal 77's placement decision

The problem

Procurement today is two tabs inside Finance (Ganit), gated on require_module("ganit"). Proposal 77 put it there to avoid minting a module code. The consequences:

  • Invisible. No sidebar entry. A user who needs purchase orders must know to click Finance first, then scroll past invoices, clients, contacts, products, and expenses to find the 7th tab.
  • Ungrantable. You cannot give a procurement manager access to purchase orders without giving them access to invoices, bank reconciliation, contracts, and e-sign.
  • Unsellable. A construction firm that needs purchasing but not invoicing must buy the entire Finance module.
  • No KPIs on entry. Committed spend, budget utilisation, late suppliers, and TDS 194Q warnings are buried behind a tab inside a tab.
  • Vendor management is scattered. Vendors live in ganit.py (CRUD at lines 2426–2477) but purchase orders reference them from procurement.py. A vendor's complete picture — bills, payments, orders, receipts, three-way match — spans two modules with no unified view.

What this proposal does

Create a standalone module kray (क्रय, Sanskrit for "purchase" — the buy side of vikray/विक्रय, sell) with its own sidebar entry, its own page, its own tabs, its own entitlement, and a complete procurement scope.

Module scope: what Kray owns

Kray owns the purchase-to-pay cycle — everything from the decision to buy through receiving to the three-way match. Ganit owns what happens after: the vendor bill, the payment, and the ledger entry.

The vendor question

Decision required: Where does vendor CRUD live?

Vendors (ganit_vendors) currently live in ganit.py at three endpoints: list, create, update (lines 2426–2477). Both procurement and ganit read them. Three options:

Option A: Vendors move to Kray entirely move

Vendor CRUD moves to procurement.py, gated on kray. Ganit reads vendors (for bill creation) via a shared read endpoint or cross-module call. Pros: vendors are a purchasing concern — you buy from them. The vendor detail naturally shows POs, receipts, bills, payments. Cons: an org with ganit but not kray can no longer create vendors. Requires a shared vendor-read endpoint.

Option B: Vendors stay in Ganit, Kray reads them keep

Vendor CRUD stays in ganit.py. Kray's Vendors tab is a read + enriched view (vendor list with PO data, spend data, three-way match) that calls /v1/ganit/vendors for the base list and enriches with procurement data. Pros: no migration of ownership. Ganit remains self-sufficient. Cons: two modules rendering the same entity. Creating a vendor from inside Kray means calling ganit's endpoint.

Option C: Vendors become shared infrastructure shared

Vendor CRUD moves to a shared router (/api/v1/vendors) gated on kray OR ganit — either module entitles vendor management. Both Kray and Ganit render vendor views in their own context. Pros: cleanest ownership — vendors are a first-class entity, not an appendage. Cons: new router, new endpoint prefix.

Recommendation: Option A. A vendor is fundamentally who you buy from. Bills and payments are what you owe them. The ownership line is: Kray owns the vendor and the order; Ganit owns the bill and the payment. An org that has neither module has no vendors.

What stays in Ganit

  • Invoices — outbound billing, the receivables side.
  • Clients & contacts — CRM overlap; stays with invoices.
  • Products catalogue — shared between invoices and POs via /v1/products. Neither module "owns" it; it stays where it is.
  • Expenses — employee expense claims, not vendor purchases.
  • Contracts — document management, not procurement.
  • E-sign — document signing; unrelated.
  • Collections — receivables follow-up.
  • Recurring invoices — outbound billing automation.
  • Bank reconciliation — reads vendor payments but doesn't own the vendor.
  • Timesheet — time tracking for billing.
  • Stats & Analytics — DSO, collection rates, ageing.

After the move, GanitPage drops from 16 tabs to 12 tabs: invoices, clients, contacts, products, expenses, contracts, e-sign, collections, recurring, bank, timesheet, stats/analytics.

  • Move vendor CRUD from ganit.py (3 endpoints: list, create, update)
  • Move vendor bill CRUD from ganit.py (list, detail, create, record payment)
  • New endpoint: GET /v1/procurement/vendors/{id}/summary — unified vendor view with PO count, open orders, billed total, paid total, outstanding, three-way match health
  • New endpoint: GET /v1/procurement/reports/vendor-spend — spend by vendor with YTD, MTD, open commitments
  • New endpoint: GET /v1/procurement/reports/po-ageing — open POs bucketed by age (current, 1-30, 31-60, 61-90, 90+ days)

ganit.py

  • Remove vendor CRUD (lines 2426–2477) — ONLY if Option A is chosen
  • Remove vendor bill CRUD (lines 2497–2663) — bills belong with the vendor
  • Keep vendor payment recording in bank reconciliation context (the payment already exists, bank just matches it)
  • Vendor reads for invoice creation: add a thin read-only proxy or accept that the frontend calls the kray endpoint

Approval levels

The existing approval design is preserved unchanged. Approval is NOT gated on require_level("kray", APPROVER). The PO approval rule names specific people in settings — the module entitlement gate would refuse the very people the firm nominated. may_approve enforces the firm's own rule; the module gate only checks the viewer has kray access at all.

Editor-level gating

Same position as ganit: ordinary writes are NOT raised to editor level. Every existing ganit grant predates the level column or defaults to viewer. Since kray entitlements are seeded by copying ganit rows, the same problem applies. Tightening needs the grant-level backfill first.

Migration plan

Staging and production share one database. Entitlement rows must exist before the gate changes. Order is non-negotiable.

Migration 214a — seed module entitlements. INSERT kray into staging.module_subscriptions for every org that currently holds ganit. Additive, zero-risk.
Migration 214b — seed member entitlements. For every staging.org_member_modules row where module_code = 'ganit', INSERT a 'kray' row at the same level. Everyone who has Finance can see Procurement on day one. Also additive.
Deploy backend. require_module("kray") in procurement.py, "kray" in ALL_MODULES. Vendor + bill endpoints moved. The old ganit vendor endpoints return 404 (if removed) or redirect.
Deploy frontend. KrayPage live, GanitPage loses 3 tabs, new nav entry visible.
Shared-DB risk: Steps 1–2 are additive INSERTs — they cannot break existing behaviour. The danger window is between step 3 (backend gates on kray) and step 2 (entitlement rows exist). If the backend deploys first without the migration, /v1/procurement/* returns 403 for everyone. Always apply migration before deploying the backend.
Vendor endpoint move risk: If vendor CRUD moves from /v1/ganit/vendors to /v1/procurement/vendors, any frontend page still calling the old endpoint gets a 404. The frontend deploy must happen in the SAME deploy cycle as the backend, or the old endpoints must stay as redirects during the transition.

What this unlocks

  • Grantable independently. An org that does books but not purchasing can turn off kray without losing ganit. A construction firm can subscribe to procurement without paying for invoicing.
  • Visible in the sidebar. Procurement is no longer the 7th tab inside someone else's module.
  • Separately sellable. Plans can include/exclude kray independently, like esign or prachar.
  • Own KPI strip. Committed spend, pending approvals, late suppliers, and unpaid bills surface immediately on module entry.
  • Unified vendor view. One tab that shows everything about a vendor: orders, receipts, bills, payments, three-way match health. No more jumping between Finance tabs.
  • First-class budgets. Department budgets promoted from a settings sub-panel to a real tab with utilisation tracking and overspend alerts.
  • Consolidated reports. All procurement reports in one place: committed spend, GRNI accrual, late suppliers, TDS 194Q, vendor spend analysis, PO ageing.
  • Ganit gets simpler. Finance drops from 16 tabs to 12. The remaining tabs are purely about invoicing, collections, banking, and the outbound money.

Naming decision

kharidखरीदHindi/Urdu, not Sanskrit. Breaks the naming pattern.
procurement—English. Every other module code is transliterated Sanskrit.
kray-vikray pairक्रय-विक्रयThe pair is a common Hindi phrase meaning "buying and selling" / "trade". The symmetry is immediately obvious to any Hindi speaker.

Recommendation: kray — it is the buy side of vikray (sell), which is exactly what procurement is. The pairing is both etymologically correct and immediately recognisable.

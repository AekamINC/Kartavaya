2What the licence actually says

Vercel's Fair Use guidance and plan terms limit Hobby to personal, non-commercial projects. The disallowed list includes, in substance:

  • Anything that generates revenue — SaaS, e-commerce, paid services
  • Advertising or affiliate content
  • Deploying on behalf of an organisation rather than yourself
  • More than one person collaborating — Hobby is single-seat, Pro is priced per seat

Kartavaya is a subscription product sold by Aekam Inc to Indian firms. It fails the first and third tests outright, and will fail the fourth the moment anyone else needs deploy access.

What enforcement looks like in practice

Vercel does not litigate. What happens is an automated or manual flag, then an email requiring an upgrade within a short window, then suspension of the deployment. The risk is not a legal bill — it is kartavaya.com going dark on someone else's timetable, with paying customers on it and no SLA to appeal to.

Hobby also carries operational limits you would not choose for a customer-facing product regardless of the licence: 100 GB bandwidth/month, no SLA, no support channel, and no password protection on preview deployments.

Why not Railway

Tempting — you already pay always-on compute for two environments, and it would kill the cross-origin surface. But your users are in India and Railway has no India region; the DB and backend are in Singapore. Serving 7.2 MB of assets from Singapore instead of Mumbai is a first-paint regression on every page load, for every customer, forever. The frontend is the one part of this stack that genuinely benefits from an edge CDN. Rejected.

5Recommendation

Move the frontend to Cloudflare Pages. It is licensed for commercial use on the free tier, it has Indian PoPs where your customers are, it consolidates onto a vendor already in the stack (R2 holds your uploads), and the one Vercel-specific piece gets simpler, not harder.

Immediately, before any migration: the licence exposure is live today and the migration is half a day away. If you want the risk gone tonight, upgrade to Pro for one month (₹1,750) and migrate unhurried. That is the cheap insurance and I would take it.

Why the OG function gets better

Today's design is a UA-sniffing rewrite in vercel.json, and the comment in api/og.js is candid that the cleaner alternative was rejected only because it meant "reproducing Vite's hashed asset names in a file that is not built by Vite". Cloudflare Pages Functions have an ASSETS binding — the function can hand a human the real, freshly-built index.html without knowing anything about hashed filenames. The reason for the sniffing hack disappears.

// frontend/functions/i/[token].js
const CRAWLER = /(whatsapp|facebookexternalhit|facebookcatalog|twitterbot|telegrambot|
slackbot|linkedinbot|discordbot|skypeuripreview|embedly|bitlybot|googlebot)/i;

export async function onRequest({ request, params, env }) {
  const ua = request.headers.get('user-agent') || '';
  if (!CRAWLER.test(ua)) {
    // A human. Serve the built SPA shell — no hashed-filename guessing.
    return env.ASSETS.fetch(new URL('/index.html', request.url));
  }
  return ogCard(params.token, env);   // the body of today's api/og.js, unchanged
}

The refusal behaviour — a draft, cancelled or settled invoice producing the same generic card as an unknown token — is inside ogCard and carries over untouched. That property is deliberate and must survive the move; it is the reason a card cannot confirm a guessed token.

6Certificates

SSL costs nothing on either platform and is automatic on both. It is not a factor in this decision.

Adding kartavaya.com as a custom domain to a Pages project makes Cloudflare issue the certificate (Google Trust Services or Let's Encrypt), install it, and renew it — no action, no expiry to track, no fee. Identical to what Vercel does today.

With the nameservers at Cloudflare you additionally get Universal SSL, which covers the apex plus *.kartavaya.com in one certificate. Vercel issues one per domain you add; Cloudflare covers the zone. Given the wildcard record described below, that is the better fit. The limit is that Universal SSL wildcards one level only — *.kartavaya.com is covered, *.*.kartavaya.com is not, which does not affect anything you run.

Two settings that must be right.

SSL/TLS mode must be "Full (strict)". Some Cloudflare zones default to "Flexible", which terminates TLS at the edge and speaks plain HTTP to the origin. For a static Pages site the origin is Cloudflare itself, so it is moot — but the moment anything is proxied through to Railway, Flexible silently downgrades that leg. Set Full (strict) on day one and it never becomes a question.

The HSTS header is preload-flagged. frontend/vercel.json sends max-age=63072000; includeSubDomains; preload. includeSubDomains means every subdomain must serve valid HTTPS, and if the domain has actually been submitted to the browser preload list that rule is baked into browsers and is slow and painful to reverse. It is not a reason to avoid Cloudflare — Universal SSL covers subdomains — but it means no subdomain may be left on plain HTTP at any point during the cutover.

Both platforms are reverse proxies that terminate TLS at the edge and hold the private key. Neither gives end-to-end encryption where only your server can decrypt. No change either way; worth knowing rather than worth worrying about.

7The domain moves too — and this is the real work

I had scoped this as "repoint DNS". That was wrong, and it is the reason the estimate moves. Vercel is your DNS provider. The nameservers for kartavaya.com are ns1.vercel-dns.com and ns2.vercel-dns.com, so the entire zone lives inside the account you are leaving. Leaving Vercel means rebuilding the zone somewhere else, not editing one record.

The good news, measured

There is no email on this domain. kartavaya.com has no MX, no SPF, no DKIM and no DMARC record. Your mail runs on unicodegroup.com, which is registered elsewhere entirely — nameservers ns*.ui-dns.* at IONOS, MX at mx00/mx01.ionos.co.uk. Nothing about this migration can touch email, which removes the single most dangerous failure mode a DNS move normally carries.

What remains in the zone is small: an apex A, www, and a wildcard *.kartavaya.com pointing at Vercel. The wildcard is why staging, app, api and even zzq7x-nonexistent.kartavaya.com all resolve — they are not real records, they are the wildcard answering. That wildcard must be recreated deliberately, or every subdomain you have casually relied on stops resolving at once.

I cannot see the whole zone from outside, and neither can Cloudflare's importer. A wildcard masks which subdomains have real records, and TXT verification tokens sit on names I would have to guess. Step one is exporting the zone file from the Vercel dashboard — that is the only authoritative list. Do not let Cloudflare's auto-scan be the source of truth; reconcile its result against the export, line by line.

Side finding: the CSP points at the wrong domain

frontend/vercel.json allows connect-src https://staging.kartavya.com — kartavya, not kartavaya. That is the misspelling that has come up before. The effect is that the CSP permits connections to a domain that is not yours, and does not permit them to staging.kartavaya.com, which is. Worth fixing in the same pass, independently of hosting.

Registrar — resolved, and it is good news

whois is not available on this machine, so I flagged this as the one open risk: if the domain had been bought through Vercel it would need transferring out first, and transfer locks run in days.

Confirmed by Keval, 8 August 2026: the domain is ours, registered directly. Vercel operates the nameservers but does not hold the registration. Changing nameservers is a setting we edit at the registrar whenever we like — no transfer, no 60-day lock, no dependency on Vercel's cooperation. Nothing remains that can move the cutover date.

What still matters is only that the registrar login is to hand on the day, and that the domain is unlocked for nameserver edits. Both are on the Monday list.

8The plan

  1. Buy a month of Pro. Removes the licence exposure today. Everything below then happens without a clock running. (5 minutes, ₹1,750.)
  2. Delete the root vercel.json. First confirm which of the two is actually served, by reading the Content-Security-Policy response header from kartavaya.com live. Keep the one in force; delete the other. Do not merge them — the weaker CSP must not win. (30 minutes.)
  3. Translate the config. frontend/public/_headers gets the cache rules, the CSP, HSTS, X-Frame-Options, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy. frontend/public/_redirects gets /* /index.html 200. The crawler rule does not go here — it becomes the function above. (1–2 hours.)
  4. Port api/og.js to functions/i/[token].js as sketched. (1 hour.)
  5. Swap analytics. Drop @vercel/analytics and the inject() call in src/index.jsx; enable Cloudflare Web Analytics. Then remove va.vercel-scripts.com and vitals.vercel-insights.com from the CSP script-src/connect-src. Cloudflare Web Analytics is cookieless — a quiet improvement to the DPDP posture, since it removes a third-party beacon from every page. (45 minutes.)
  6. Re-enter env vars in the Pages project — eight of them: VITE_BACKEND_URL, VITE_SUPABASE_URL, VITE_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY, VITE_ENVIRONMENT, VITE_PAY_BASE_URL, VITE_LEAD_CTA_HREF, VITE_AEKAM_STATE_CODE, plus BACKEND_URL for the function. Production and preview separately. (30 minutes.)
  7. Deploy to a *.pages.dev URL and test there — before any DNS change. Verify: SPA deep links, the CSP header byte-for-byte, service worker scope, a real WhatsApp preview on a live share token, and Supabase realtime over wss://. (1–2 hours.)
  8. Export the Vercel zone file. This is the authoritative record list; everything downstream is reconciled against it, because Cloudflare's auto-scan cannot see through the wildcard. Have the registrar login to hand and confirm the domain is unlocked for nameserver edits. (30 minutes, do this first.)
  9. Rebuild the zone at Cloudflare. Add the site, let the auto-scan run, then reconcile against the export line by line — the scan cannot see through the wildcard. Recreate the apex, www, and *.kartavaya.com explicitly. Set SSL/TLS to Full (strict). Do not change nameservers yet. (1–2 hours.)
  10. Verify the new zone before it is live by querying Cloudflare's assigned nameservers directly — nslookup kartavaya.com <cf-ns> — and diff every answer against the current live zone. This is the step that makes the cutover boring. (45 minutes.)
  11. Switch nameservers at the registrar. The zone TTL is already 600s, but NS records at the TLD carry a 48-hour TTL — resolvers will serve both old and new for up to two days. Both must answer correctly throughout, which is exactly why the Vercel project stays paid and live. Cut on a weekend morning IST. (30 minutes + up to 48h propagation.)
  12. Cancel Pro and delete the Vercel project once traffic has been clean for a full week after propagation completes — not after the switch.

Revised estimate: one full day of work, spread over about a week of elapsed time, plus one month of Pro as insurance. The half-day figure in my first draft assumed DNS was a repoint. It is a zone rebuild, and the 48-hour NS propagation window is not compressible.

Target of mid next week is comfortable provided steps 1 and 2 happen at the start of the week — the zone can be built and verified days before the nameservers move, with no customer impact, because nothing is live until the registrar switch.

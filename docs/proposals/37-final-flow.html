Why the card matters

A PDF attachment shows no preview and downloads a file most people never find again. The card tells the client what this is before they tap.

It is also visible to anyone the chat is forwarded to. That is the reason for the next screen.

What the doorstep does and does not say

Shown: who sent it, the invoice number, the due date, the amount, and who was billed — the same facts WhatsApp already put in the preview card, so the doorstep leaks nothing the chat did not.

Held back until they tap: every line item, HSN codes, quantities, rates, both GSTINs, the delivery address and the payment history. A forwarded chat does not spill your client's order book to whoever it reached.

Two doors, not one. Someone who only wants to pay presses Pay and never reads the invoice; someone who wants to check it first presses View. Neither is buried under the other.

If you want it tighter, this screen is the natural place to ask for the last four digits of the client's phone before revealing the lines — one field, no account.

04 View invoice — the whole thing, sized to the screen

Laptop puts the invoice first with paying as a rail. Phone puts paying first, because someone who already decided should not scroll past fourteen lines to do it.

The two things this flow still needs built

Server-rendered Open Graph tags on /i/:token. WhatsApp's crawler does not run JavaScript, so a plain SPA route produces no preview card and step 02 would not happen. That path needs an edge function rendering per-invoice meta tags plus a generated thumbnail image.

A pay_token column on ganit_invoices. Short, opaque, random, unique — never the invoice UUID. Additive, but staging and production share one database, so it lands in production the moment it is applied.

Two corrections to proposals 77 and 78

1. Departments ARE real records. I said twice that department is free text and not a modelled entity. Half wrong. manav_departments exists, holds 30 rows, and carries a head_employee_id — an owner, which is precisely what a budget needs. What is missing is only the link: manav_employees.department is TEXT with no foreign key. And 86 of 98 employees carry a department string, of which 85 already match a real department row by name.

So budget-by-department and approval-by-department are far more viable than I said. The job is a foreign key and one data fix for a single mismatched row — not modelling departments from scratch.

2. The Labour Codes are already in force. All four commenced 21 November 2025. That is not a forthcoming change to plan for; it is law that has been operative for nine months, and it changes how every salary in this product must be structured.

1 · Invoice

What the law requires now

RuleDetailBites when
E-invoicing (IRN) Mandatory at ₹5 crore aggregate turnover, counted from any year since FY 2017-18. The moment a client crosses ₹5cr — and most CA-firm clients will.
The 30-day rule From 1 April 2025, a business at ₹10cr+ must report an invoice to the IRP within 30 days of the invoice date. After that the IRP rejects it and no IRN is generated. Credit and debit notes are in scope too. Day 31. There is no appeal and no late path.
Cancellation window An IRN can be cancelled within 24 hours. Miss it and the only remedy is a credit note, itself reported to the IRP. Hour 25.

What we have

ack_no, ack_date missing The IRP's acknowledgement cannot be stored, so there is no proof of when we reported — which is the only defence against the 30-day rule.
signed_qr / qr_code missing The signed QR is mandatory on the printed invoice. Without it the document is not a compliant e-invoice however correct the figures are.
einvoice_statusmissing No way to tell "not applicable" from "not yet reported" from "rejected" — and those are three completely different problems.
eway_bill_nomissing No e-way bill linkage at all.
original_invoice_idmissing The one that matters most. A credit note cannot say which invoice it credits. GST requires that reference, the IRP requires it, and without it a credit note is an orphan document.

The architectural read

E-invoicing here is a stub, not a feature: one column with no state machine, no acknowledgement, no QR and no failure handling. That is worse than not having it, because the column implies a capability that does not exist.

And credit notes are functionally incomplete. They can be created — that shipped this week — but they cannot reference the invoice they reverse. Given the 24-hour IRN cancellation window, the credit note is the correction mechanism for e-invoicing. It has to carry the link before e-invoicing is worth starting.

2 · Procurement

Nothing exists. No purchase order, no requisition, no goods receipt, no anything. The landing zone does: ganit_vendor_bills (189 rows) and ganit_vendors (80).

Fully specified in proposal 77; competitive position in 78. Nothing to add here except the architect's note: the PO is the only object in the system that records a future obligation. Everything else — invoice, bill, payment, payslip — records something that already happened. That is why it cannot be a field on the vendor bill, and it is also why committed spend is currently unanswerable.

3 · Payroll

The Labour Codes changed the base

In force since 21 November 2025, replacing 29 laws with four codes. The change that reaches every payslip:

Basic + DA + retaining allowance must be at least 50% of total remuneration. If allowances exceed 50%, the excess is automatically reclassified as wages for PF, gratuity, ESI and bonus.

Two more that change the arithmetic:

  • Fixed-term staff get gratuity after 1 year — no five-year wait.
  • Full and final settlement within two working days of the last working day, under Section 17(2) of the Code on Wages. Not "by the next payroll run". Two days.

Why the missing gross on the salary structure matters

93 salary structures exist and all 93 carry basic. None carries gross or ctc.

Which means basic-as-a-percentage-of-gross is not computable from the structure — and that is exactly the test the Code on Wages now imposes. We cannot tell a firm whether its salary structures comply with the 50% rule, because the denominator is not stored. It can be summed from the components, but a sum is not the same as a declared gross, and the reclassification rule turns on the declared figure.

This is one column and a check. It is also the single highest-value payroll change available, because every firm in India is currently restructuring salaries because of it — and nobody in this market is telling them whether they got it right.

Full and final settlement

We have manav_offboarding (11 rows) and manav_exit_interviews. We do not have an F&F computation. What one has to contain, from the research:

  • Unpaid salary to the last working day, plus arrears
  • Pro-rata bonus — owed to anyone who worked 30+ days in the year
  • Leave encashment
  • Gratuity where eligible
  • Pro-rata annual allowances (LTA, medical) to the last working day
  • Recoveries — notice shortfall, advances, unreturned assets
  • TDS under s.192 computed on the full year's income including the F&F month, before payment is released

Two traps the sources name explicitly: using ÷30 for earnings and ÷26 for recovery (or the reverse) without contractual backing invites disputes; and the employee who left at 4 years 9 months — the gratuity edge case everyone gets wrong.

We have manav_assets with 100 rows, so the "F&F held because HR is waiting for the laptop" case is already modellable. That is a real advantage — most payroll tools cannot see assets at all.

Attendance, leave with types and balances, shifts and schedules, recruitment, onboarding, assets, offboarding, exit interviews, expense claims, departments with heads. Against the competitive set in proposal 78, the coverage is comparable to Keka and broader than greytHR. What Darwinbox adds beyond this — LMS, succession, workforce analytics — is the part explicitly called overspecified for a firm this size.

And it all hangs off one employee record, which is the structural advantage Zoho does not have.

The one HR gap that blocks other things

manav_employees.department is TEXT with no foreign key to manav_departments, even though the table exists and 85 of 86 named employees already match a real row.

It blocks three separate things: PO budgets by department, PO approval routing by department, and the commission revenue_scope: 'department' that shipped this week and currently resolves against a string. One foreign key, one data fix, three unblocked features.

The statute calendar — where the gap really is

45 rows across 37 obligation keys, and the coverage is genuinely good on the tax side: GST returns and thresholds and rates, e-invoice threshold, ITC time limit and reversal, TDS deposit and statements and certificates, advance tax by quarter, EPF and ESI remittance, MSME payment disallowance.

On the labour side there is exactly one key: esi.wage_ceiling.

Nothing for gratuity, nothing for the Payment of Bonus Act, nothing for LWF, and nothing for the wage definition that came into force in November 2025. The mechanism is right — dated law, read as_of, which is better architecture than any competitor's hardcoded rates. It is simply not populated for the half of the law that governs payroll.

This is also the honest answer to "where are we behind greytHR". Not the architecture — the content, and the process that keeps it current.

The architect's summary, in five lines

  • HR is the strongest domain and needs one foreign key, not a rebuild.
  • Payroll computes salary, not payroll — no gratuity, bonus, LWF, arrears or encashment, and it cannot test the wage rule that has governed Indian salaries since November 2025.
  • Invoicing is compliant for a normal invoice and incomplete for the two documents that matter under stress: the credit note and the e-invoice.
  • Procurement does not exist, and is the only place a future obligation could ever be recorded.
  • The statute calendar is the best idea in the product — dated law rather than hardcoded rates — and it is half empty.

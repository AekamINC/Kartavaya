Compliance is a setting

The owner's rule: no government requirement is ever mandatory in the product — each module carries settings where the firm ticks what applies to it. Here is how to build that so it never quietly produces a wrong document, what each module's settings should be, and the one place the rule cannot hold.

Proposal 80 · 2026-08-22 · schema audited read-only · Pahchan researched against the DPDP Act and its 2025 Rules

The pattern already exists — in Pahchan

pahchan_policy is exactly the table this proposal is about, built for one module and never generalised. Per org it carries geofence radius, grace minutes, whether punching outside a geofence is allowed, an accuracy threshold, photo retention in days, record retention in years, report recipients and cadence, standard hours, three overtime settings, week start and shift times.

And the two live orgs genuinely differ: one allows punching outside the geofence, the other does not; one keeps records 5 years, the other 3.

So the answer to "how should this work" is: like Pahchan, everywhere. What follows is that pattern generalised, plus the two rules that stop it becoming a way to make warnings disappear.

Three states, not two

A tick-box has two states and that is one too few. "Off" cannot distinguish this does not apply to us from we have not got to it yet, and those demand opposite behaviour from the product.

Not applicable
The firm states this does not apply — a composition dealer charging no GST, a practice with no employees under ESI. Field hidden. No warning. Nothing counted as missing, because nothing is.
Applicable  default
It applies, and we never stop you. The field is shown, optional, and the product names the consequence of leaving it empty: "no HSN — the buyer's input credit may be questioned". Nothing is blocked. Ever.
Enforced
The firm chooses the guardrail, because it would rather be stopped than be wrong. Only reachable by someone deliberately ticking it.

The default is always the middle one. Nothing arrives enforced.

The two rules that keep it honest

1 · "Not applicable" is a decision, not an absence

Who set it, when, and optionally why. Without that, six months later nobody can tell "we are a composition dealer so GST does not apply" from "someone unticked it to make a warning go away". One is a fact about the business; the other is a problem hiding.

2 · Freeze the profile onto the document

This one is not optional, and the product already has the pattern: vetana_payslips.statutory_treatment records how each payslip was computed, so a March settings change cannot restate January.

Invoices need the same. A document issued while HSN was not applicable must keep reading that way for ever. Otherwise switching a setting on retroactively makes a year of correctly-issued invoices look non-compliant — and switching one off makes a year of genuinely deficient ones look clean. Both are worse than the original problem.

Pahchan — the research, and the one finding

What the law says

  • The DPDP Rules 2025 were notified on 13 November 2025. The framework is operative, not forthcoming.
  • Biometric data is classified as sensitive personal data. Processing requires explicit, informed consent.
  • It cannot be a condition of employment. Consent given under employment pressure is expressly identified as not free consent.
  • A reasonable alternative must be offered — an ID badge is the example given — to any employee who declines.
  • Encryption, access control and secure storage are required; retention is limited to the purpose, after which the data must be securely erased.

What we have — and it is better than I expected

ControlState
No biometric template is stored good The audit searched every column in the schema for a template, embedding, descriptor or bytea. There is none. Pahchan stores a reference photo and a punch photo — images, not a mathematical template. Legally a materially smaller exposure.
Retention is configured good punch_photo_retention_days (90) and record_retention_years (3–5), per org, and the two live orgs have chosen different values.
A versioned notice exists good pahchan_notice_acknowledgements records the notice version, the device clock as the legally relevant moment, the source, and whether it happened offline. That is careful work.
Geofence and accuracy are recorded per punch good lat, lng, accuracy, distance, mock-location flag, and a review verdict. Enough to defend or dispute any single punch.

The finding

Consent is recorded against a USER ACCOUNT. The people being photographed are EMPLOYEES. Most employees have no account.

Measured on the live database:

  • 12 employees of a real org (Unicode Group) have enrollment photos, and 699 punches exist against them.
  • 3 acknowledgement rows exist in the entire product — one per org, each by an administrator account. Every one has employee_id = NULL.
  • So zero of the twelve enrolled people have a consent record against them in this system.

The acknowledgement is keyed (org_id, user_id, notice_version), which records that the operator of the app saw the notice — appropriate for a self-service punch, and not the same thing as the data principal consenting. Under the DPDP Act the data principal is the employee whose face is stored.

And there is no opt-out anywhere. The schema was searched for any column meaning declined, exempt, opted-out or alternative. Outside eSign, there is none. The law requires that an employee can refuse and be given another way to mark attendance; today the product cannot record that they did.

Stated fairly: consent may well exist on paper — bulk enrolment with a paper code was a deliberate design. The point is not that the firm is non-compliant. It is that the system cannot show consent, and if asked, could not produce it.

What the market says the problem is

  • 74% of employers report payroll losses to buddy punching, averaging 2.2% of gross payroll. The framing that explains why: every technology that records only when a punch happened — punch cards, swipe cards, PINs, web check-in — captures a timestamp, and a timestamp cannot tell you who was standing there.
  • GPS spoofing is no longer theoretical. Fake-GPS apps are free and take about thirty seconds to set up on Android. Detection in production catches an estimated 60–80% of attempts.
  • Identity and location verified together is the only combination that closes the gap for field teams — location says where the phone is, the face says who is holding it.

Pahchan already does both together, with a geofence, an accuracy reading, a distance, a mock-location flag and a photo on every punch. On capability this is not behind.

The one real difference — and it should be a setting

The market draws a sharp line: “an app that logs a warning when it detects spoofing is an audit tool. An app that blocks the check-in entirely is an enforcement tool.” And greytHR’s published answer to “can someone spoof GPS to mark fake attendance?” is unambiguous: no — the employee is blocked from signing in entirely, and spoofed data cannot be submitted.

Pahchan does the opposite, on purpose. It flags and never blocks. The rule is stated in the code itself, and the reasoning is better than the market’s:

“The client used to HIDE THE SHUTTER at three, which contradicted this module’s own rule that nothing blocks a punch — three camera errors in a dark doorway locked someone out of clocking in entirely.”

On a missing reference photo: “it does not stop the employee being paid”. On repeated retries: “asks a manager to look at the day; it does not refuse the punch and is not an accusation. A bad front camera produces this exactly as readily as someone hunting for a frame that hides where they are.”

That is the right default and the more humane product: the cost of a false positive is a person who cannot clock in and may not be paid. But a firm losing 2.2% of payroll to buddy punching will reasonably want the enforcement tool — and today we cannot give it to them at any price.

So: on_mock_location = flag (default) | block. Exactly the shape allow_outside_geofence already has. The owner’s rule survives intact — nothing blocks unless a firm ticks the box — and the competitive gap closes.

Plus one schema change that is not a setting: pahchan_notice_acknowledgements.employee_id already exists and is nullable. Populate it. The column was built for exactly this and has never been used.

Custom attendance policies — and who gets to see them

Truein’s headline is 70+ customisable attendance policies. That number is not seventy concepts: it is roughly ten policy families multiplied by the levels they can be applied at. Their levels are site, staff category and role.

The shape already exists here, in one dimension

pahchan_policy holds about seventeen org-level settings, and pahchan_sites.radius_m already overrides pahchan_policy.default_radius_m per site. That is a resolution chain in miniature, working today for exactly one setting.

Generalised, the chain is:

org default  →  site  →  staff category  →  employee

First match wins — the same rule the PO approval matrix uses in proposal 77, and the same reason: an ordered list is something a person can read aloud, and a graph is not.

The ten families: shift timing, grace, late mark, half-day threshold, early out, overtime, geofence, photo requirement, regularisation window, retention. Seven of the ten are already settable at org level. So this is not a new system — it is a scope column and one resolver.

The half that matters: what the EMPLOYEE sees

Today the employee sees verdicts, not rules. AttendanceHistory shows late / present / needs review, and its own comment says why it cannot do better:

“asserting the org’s shift policy, and it would be wrong for every six-day week”

The client does not have the policy, so it cannot explain anything. There is exactly one place with the right instinct — ClockScreen already says “Your device reports a simulated location. This punch will be flagged prominently.” That is the pattern; it just lives in one screen and covers one condition.

The change is to send the resolved policy to the device — not the raw configuration, the answer for this person at this site — and render it as plain sentences.

Why this is worth more than the policy engine itself

  1. “Why was I marked late?” is the first support question in every attendance product. Answered on the device, the ticket never opens.
  2. DPDP transparency. An employee whose face and location are being processed should be able to see the rules they are judged by. Today they cannot, and that sits awkwardly beside a consent notice that says what we collect but not what we do with it.
  3. Nobody else does it. Truein, greytHR and Spintly all show the ADMIN the policy and the EMPLOYEE the verdict. Showing the employee the rule is a small change that no competitor has made.

What it costs

  • A scope column on the policy settings (org / site / category / employee) and one resolver — the sites table and the org policy already exist.
  • One endpoint returning the resolved policy for the caller, so the device holds the answer rather than the configuration.
  • The sentences on three existing mobile screens.

Small, and it pairs naturally with the two consent items — all three touch the same screens, and all three are about telling the employee something the system already knows.

Where the rule cannot hold

Two things I would make hard to switch off rather than impossible — off with a typed confirmation and a recorded reason, so the decision is deliberate and attributable:

  • Invoice number and date. Without them the document cannot be referenced in a return or in a credit note. It is not an invoice; it is a note. The code refuses this today (_refuse_final_if_incomplete) and drafts stay permissive, which is the right shape — an incomplete draft is the workflow, an incomplete FINAL is a lie.
  • The e-invoicing 30-day clock. Day 31 is rejected by the IRP with no late path. A firm that switches this warning off does not escape the rule; it only stops being told.

Everything else — HSN, GSTIN, place of supply, PF, ESI, PT, gratuity, bonus, LWF, the wage rule, biometric consent — optional, with the consequence stated. Exactly as asked.

How to build it once

staging.module_compliance_settings
    org_id, module, rule_key,
    state          'not_applicable' | 'applicable' | 'enforced'
    set_by, set_at, reason
    UNIQUE (org_id, module, rule_key)

One table, one resolver, every module. A rule with no row resolves to applicable, so nothing needs seeding and nothing arrives enforced. Pahchan keeps pahchan_policy for its operational numbers — radius, grace, hours — because those are not compliance states; only the applies/does-not-apply questions move here.

The frozen copy on each document is a jsonb column, alongside the statutory_treatment that already does this for payslips.

What I would do first

  1. Populate employee_id on the Pahchan acknowledgement. The column exists. This is the only item in this document with a live legal exposure attached.
  2. Add the opt-out path. Required by law, absent from the schema.
  3. Build the settings table and resolver, and move the Ganit rules onto it.
  4. Freeze the profile onto invoices, as payslips already do.
  5. Give the remaining modules their settings screens as each is next touched.

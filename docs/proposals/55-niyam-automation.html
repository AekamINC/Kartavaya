Niyam

A fresh automation system for Kartavaya, designed from first principles to replace the entire existing estate. Zero AI model calls anywhere in it — every trigger, condition and action is deterministic. Written 2026-08-16 from a re-audit of all eight automation surfaces against today's code, and a three-architecture design panel judged on buildability and physics safety. Supersedes proposals 41, 42 and 43 as plans; they remain the record of what was wrong. Niyam — नियम, rule — sits beside Graha, Vikray, Ganit, Vetana.

What changed, and why this is a replacement rather than a repair. The previous plan (A1→A10) was incremental: fix the CRM log, then build a spine beside the two existing engines. The re-audit found the estate is worse than that plan assumed — two engines both named fire_automations that share nothing but the name, two builders selling triggers that never fire, a third UI (Varta auto-replies) with no engine behind it at all, twenty cron dispatchers across five surfaces, and an action layer where five handlers crash on invented columns. There is nothing here worth carrying forward. The one genuinely good asset — the config-parity ratchet — is a pattern, not code, and it is reused below.

2 · The physics this design obeys

These are not preferences. A design that violates one of them fails in production, and three of them have already cost this product an incident.

  • Production co-writes the database. Staging and production share one Supabase Postgres, and production runs main — 1,144 commits behind. Anything installed as a DB row-trigger fires from production too, blind, with no actor and no source. ⇒ DB triggers are rejected as the event source.
  • PgBouncer transaction pooling kills the obvious mechanisms. LISTEN/NOTIFY does not work. SET LOCAL and session GUCs do not survive to a trigger — even SET search_path is best-effort. Multiple gunicorn workers. ⇒ transport is a polled outbox table; actor and source are explicit columns, never session state.
  • Quiet hours are honoured only by accident. prefs_allow gates only sends routed through push_service; the reminder cron, the approvals fan-out and the whole action layer bypass it. ⇒ one gated send API, and no way to reach a provider around it.
  • "Sent" currently means "handed to a thread". The provider's real answer exists only in staging.outbound_log. ⇒ delivery evidence is a foreign key into that table, never a boolean the engine stores about itself.
  • Every migration touches production data. ⇒ the suppression switch ships before the first table, not with it.

3 · Event capture — app-emitted, transactional, one choke-point

One emitter, called from the lowest shared service mutator of each domain — never from a route. This is the fix for the leak the audit found: today 2 of 5 lead-creation writers and 2 of 4 task-status writers emit nothing, so the Kanban drag (the most common status change in the product) fires no rules at all. A route cannot write a watched column without passing through the service that emits.

staging.niyam_eventsWhy
event_id BIGINT GENERATED ALWAYS AS IDENTITYA monotonic cursor. Same reasoning as outbound_log: a random uuid scatters every insert across the index on the highest-write table.
org_id uuid NOT NULLTenancy on the event itself; the sweep loops orgs the way _for_each_org already does.
event_type text, entity_type text, entity_id textTEXT, not uuid — tasks.task_id is task_<hex12>. Two migrations already exist because that was forgotten before.
actor_id text NULL, source textExplicit columns, CHECK-allowlisted ('app','import','sweep','cron'). The engine refuses an app event with a NULL actor — that is the production-co-write defence, and it is a constraint rather than a convention.
payload jsonb — typed before/after snapshotThis is the fix for the bug that made every priority condition unevaluable: callers passed {task_id, team_id} and nothing else, so the engine had nothing to compare.
dedupe_key text, claimed_at, processed_atWindowed de-duplication for synthetic time events, and the drain cursor.

Guarded by a build check, not a sweep — the house rule. test_niyam_emission_parity.py enumerates every writer of every watched table and column and fails the build if one does not route through an emitting service. Grep finds only what you thought of; a ratchet finds what you forgot.

4 · The engine

Five tables, all staging.-qualified — never bare, because the startup DDL still creates four shadow tables unqualified and the empty copies win the lookup. A wait is a column, not a table: the minimalist architect's governor applied to the reliability architect's schema.

TableHolds
niyam_rulesenabled and is_armed as separate booleans — Prachar's draft-vs-scheduled split, the one production-grade idea in the old estate. A rule can exist, match, and record what it would have done, without acting.
niyam_rule_stepsAn ordered linear pipeline of condition | action | wait. Not a graph: no branching means no orphaned-node class of bug, and no flow library (there is none in package.json).
niyam_runsUNIQUE(rule_id, event_id) — idempotency by claim-by-insert. Plus dry_run bool and wake_at timestamptz for waits.
niyam_run_stepsPer-step outcome ok | refused | failed | skipped | dry, and outbound_id — a foreign key into staging.outbound_log, which is the only record of whether anything was sent.
niyam_eventsAbove.

Concurrency: workers drain with … WHERE processed_at IS NULL ORDER BY event_id FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED LIMIT $1::int — one transaction, so it works under transaction pooling — then claim each rule/event pair with INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING RETURNING run_id. One worker wins; a redeploy mid-drain replays safely. (Note the ::int cast: an untyped parameter is an instant PgBouncer 500, and this product has already lost every credit spend to that once.)

Conditions are typed, and a broken rule is unwritable

Filters evaluate against payload.after, whose keys come from the core field registry per event type plus each team's field_definitions. Operators derive from the field's type — a date offers before/after/within, a select offers is/is-not/one-of. A condition the event cannot answer is not offered at authoring time. That is the structural version of what the old engine did at runtime: it correctly refused to fire and logged a warning naming the field — into a server log nobody reads, on a rule the UI still displayed as Active.

Time triggers without a cron per trigger

One armed sweep, POST /api/internal/niyam/sweep, does three jobs: drains the event outbox, resumes waits whose wake_at has passed, and runs a fixed set of temporal predicates that emit synthetic events with source='sweep' and a dedupe_key so one overdue invoice fires once per window. The predicates are a server-side named-query allowlist — invoices_overdue, tasks_overdue, approvals_pending, contacts_stale, quotes_expiring, contracts_expiring, items_low_stock — so the owner never writes SQL and the sweep only ever runs indexed queries somebody reviewed. Per-tick ceilings on rows and fan-out, oldest-first, next-tick-is-the-retry: copied from /cron/marketing, the only loop in the old estate that was built properly.

5 · Actions — through the service layer, never a bare UPDATE

A closed allowlist. Each verb calls the same service function a human's click calls, which is what makes automation writes stamp completed_at, log activity, notify, and re-emit events so rules can chain. The old engine's change_status was a bare UPDATE tasks SET status=$1: a task an automation completed had a NULL completion timestamp and no history entry.

VerbNote
task.set_statusThrough the transition policy, with no approver identity — a robot is not an approver.
task.assignExplicit non-empty user list. The old action defaulted to [] and unassigned everyone, then reported success.
task.add_commentPosts as a seeded system actor — today four writers post as 'system', no such user row exists, and the read path inner-joins users, so those comments are invisible to everyone.
task.set_field, crm.set_deal_stage, crm.create_follow_up, crm.add_activity, crm.assign_contactStage changes stamp won_at/lost_at because they go through the mutator that already does.
notify.sendThe one gated send. Everything below.

The one gated send

services/niyam_send.py is the only way a rule reaches a human. Every channel — email, push, in-app, WhatsApp — routes through it; it consults preferences and quiet hours, writes the outbound_log attempt row, and returns its id for the run step. Per-channel failure polarity is decided once, here: an actionable (approval, mention) fails open, because a missed approval is worse than a mistimed one; bulk-at-night fails closed, because a preferences outage must not become a 2am dunning broadcast. Today a single fetchrow failure fails open on both.

6 · The four standing rules, made structural

RuleEnforced by construction
No rule calls a model newAn import-graph ratchet fails the build if anything under services/niyam/** imports the AI router, a Gemini or OpenRouter client, Serper, or the hub. The action allowlist contains no generative verb. The engine writes no credit ledger row and appears in no MODEL_PRICING path — the engine's cost per run is ₹0 and cannot become non-zero without failing CI.
No rule moves moneyNo action writes ganit_payments or any paid state; the allowlist is closed, so adding one is a reviewed code change and not a config change. "Paid" continues to come only from bank reconciliation.
No rule bypasses quiet hoursA second import ratchet: nothing under services/niyam/** may import a raw sender (send_web_push, send_expo_push, the email primitives). The only reachable path is the gated API.
Nothing says "sent" unless it was sentA run step's delivery evidence is a foreign key into outbound_log. There is no boolean for the engine to lie with, and three states are representable: delivered, suppressed, not-recorded.

7 · The builder

A card list on the existing DataTable and k-* system — not a Jira-style canvas. There is no flow library in the frontend, so a canvas is weeks of drag, edge routing and layout persistence before a single rule runs; it can come later, on a working engine. What the builder does have is the part that matters to a non-developer owner:

  • Clone a starter template, don't build from blank. Ten to fifteen prebuilt rules an Indian firm switches on in one click are the primary surface and the acceptance spec.
  • A dry run they can watch. Before arming, the rule shows its actual recipient list: "42 invoices match — here are the first ten, and exactly who each one would email." This is the single most important feature in the design and the direct antidote to 331 suppressed reminders nobody could see.
  • Honest run history — a runs table with per-step outcomes, not a run_count integer that rises whether or not anything happened.
  • Condition pickers that cannot express a broken rule, from the typed registry.

8 · The 60-item catalogue, screened for zero AI

The test: an item survives unchanged if its trigger, conditions and action were always a typed predicate plus a template — the model was never doing anything. It is redesigned if its intent survives once an AI judgement becomes a typed threshold, a keyword map, or a weighted sum over registry fields. It is cut if the value is the generative output.

Survive unchanged — the large majority. The whole reminder, stage-change and template-send families: task done → notify requester; invoice overdue → dunning template, deduped by date bucket; deal won → onboarding checklist + thank-you; new lead from source X → assign, first follow-up, notify; approval pending > 24h → remind the approver; contract expiring in 7 days → notify the owner.

Redesigned deterministically. (1) CRM deal health AI-scoring becomes days_since_last_activity > N plus a fixed weighted sum over numeric fields — and it fronts a person rather than firing an action. (2) Varta smart auto-reply becomes an exact keyword → approved-template map with variable substitution — which also makes it a real engine action for the first time. (3) The three broken skill actions come back deterministic: escalate becomes wait + re-check + notify a named role; notify_multi becomes an explicit recipient list; document-expiry becomes a time rule on contracts_expiring.

Cut. "Suggest an assignee", in-rule summarising and drafting, report insight generation. These move to Sahayak, invoked by a human who reads the answer — never as a rule action. Cutting "suggest assignee" also fixes an audit finding: the old action never asked whom.

10 · What stays yours

  • When may scheduled mail actually leave? Still the gate. 331 of 331 reminders were suppressed; the engine never assumes an answer. Note the loaded gun: those rows are already marked sent, so going live will not resend them — but the dedup windows lapse, and one sweep tick would create ~200 fresh past-due reminders addressed to the staff member who typed the invoice, in dunning language. That is why N8 is last and why the dedupe keys are seeded from a dry run.
  • The two master flips — NIYAM_ARMED off→on, and OUTBOUND_MODE dry→live. Deliberate, watched, never global.
  • Every per-rule arm, one at a time, lowest blast radius first. Bulk and staff-addressed templates go last.
  • Enabling the WhatsApp action per org — a paid tier, gated on an approved template, and Meta bills the org rather than Aekam.

Method: eight parallel auditors re-verified every prior claim against today's code with file-and-line evidence, then three independent architects designed the whole system under the same physics sheet from different postures (reliability, minimalism, product), and a judge scored them and picked a backbone to graft onto. All three converged independently on the same spine — app-emitted outbox, no DB triggers, one sweep, typed conditions, one gated send — which is the strongest signal in this document.

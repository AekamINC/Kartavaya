Proposal 106

What five verticals demand — and what this research could not establish

A fan-out research run across pharma, discrete manufacturing, real estate, travel and interior design, verified adversarially. It answered the statutory question well and the competitive question badly. Both halves are reported here, because the failure is the more actionable of the two.

Researched 2026-09-11 · 107 agents · 25 sources fetched · 120 claims extracted, 25 verified under 3-vote adversarial review · 13 confirmed / 12 refuted · every surviving claim sourced at the foot

Read this before using any of it

11 of the 13 surviving claims are statutory. 11 of the 12 refuted claims were vendor pricing or vendor feature assertions. The run therefore establishes what Indian law demands of each vertical with high confidence, and establishes almost nothing about what incumbents charge or ship.

Kartavaya can plan its statutory scope against this research. It cannot yet plan its pricing or its competitive positioning against it.

Two of the five sectors are effectively unanswered: travel produced zero surviving claims and pharma produced one. No sector's HR / payroll / attendance, procurement or CRM column is filled at all. The comparable five-sector module grid the brief asked for cannot be built from this run and is not attempted below.

The four findings that matter

1 · Job work is a four-part subsystem, not an inventory flag High · 3-0

A job-work despatch is a distinct statutory document type. CGST Rule 45(1)–(2) requires inputs, semi-finished goods or capital goods to move to a job worker under a challan issued by the principal, carrying the particulars of Rule 55 — whose own heading is "Transportation of goods without issue of invoice". The statute classifies it as neither an invoice nor an untracked stock transfer. Four parts, all required together:

  • The Rule 55 challan — serial number, date, consignor/consignee GSTIN, HSN, quantity, taxable value, in triplicate. A High Court has upheld a penalty where the goods description was omitted, so the particulars are operative, not advisory.
  • ITC-04, challan-level and bidirectional — covering goods dispatched to and received back from the job worker.
  • Turnover-banded periodicity — half-yearly where preceding-FY aggregate turnover exceeds ₹5 crore, annual otherwise; due the 25th of the month succeeding the period. Any software describing ITC-04 as quarterly is describing pre-October-2021 law (Notification 35/2021-CT).
  • Section 143 deemed-supply ageing — see below. This is the one that bites.

2 · The section 143 clock back-dates a tax liability High · 3-0

Section 143(3)–(4) with Rule 45(4) creates a retrospective deemed supply: goods not returned within the time limit are deemed supplied by the principal to the job worker on the day they were sent out — the supply must be declared in GSTR-1, and tax plus interest becomes payable.

The consequence for design is specific: per-challan ageing is a hard requirement, with a per-consignment, configurable deadline. One year for inputs, three years for capital goods, extendable by the Commissioner by a further one/two years — and moulds, dies, jigs, fixtures and tools are excluded from the capital-goods clock entirely.

A scalar quantity_on_hand cannot express any of this. Neither can a batch column. It needs a row per challan carrying a despatch leg, a receipt leg and a clock.

3 · Property TDS is a computed document engine High · 3-0

The 194-IA base is not the agreed sale price. Three separate computations:

  • The ₹50 lakh threshold is a two-limb test on consideration and stamp duty value — deduct if either reaches ₹50 lakh.
  • The 1% is charged on whichever of the two is higher.
  • "Consideration" is statutorily defined to include club membership fee, car parking fee, electricity or water facility fee, maintenance fee and advance fee.

So a real-estate module must store stamp duty value per unit as a computation input, and must roll the ancillary demand-letter lines Indian developers bill separately into the TDS base rather than excluding them. Both are schema decisions, not report decisions.

4 · The TAN rule splits — and it touches a standing product rule Medium · 2-1

The 194-IA path is PAN-based and must not be gated behind a TAN field — section 203A is expressly disapplied for a person deducting under 194-IA.

But this holds only for the 194-IA / 26QB path. A purchase from a non-resident seller is deducted under s.195, filed on Form 27Q with challan ITNS-281, and s.195 is not exempt from s.203A.

Direct consequence for this codebase. CLAUDE.md records that validate_tds_challan refusing an ITNS-281 without a TAN is correct and must not be relaxed (s.203A, where a PAN is explicitly not a substitute). That ruling stands and is reconfirmed here for the 281 path. But it must not be reused for property TDS: a 26QB path that inherits the 281 validator would block a lawful PAN-only deduction. Two paths, two validators.

RERA — a multi-party signing workflow, not a payment entry

Escrow withdrawal requires tri-professional certification High · 3-0

An engineer, an architect and a chartered accountant in practice must each certify that a withdrawal is in proportion to the percentage of completion of the project. The certificates are retained and re-examined at annual audit, and the CA must ensure no loan is repaid directly from the RERA account.

This is a multi-party approval with attached, UDIN-bearing certificates — a direct e-sign and document-routing use case, which Kartavaya already has.

RERA reporting demands a per-allottee ledger and a fixed annual date Medium · 2-1

The HARERA Gurugram direction sets out the allottee-level table an auditor may call for: name/address, description of property sold, gross consideration per the Agreement or Letter of Allotment, prior-year cumulative collections, collections during the year, total to date, balance collectible, plus project-level totals — with the during-the-year column backed by a date-wise, per-party subsidiary statement.

The CA-certified statement of accounts must reach the Authority by 30 September, and non-submission by that date is itself a violation of s.4(2)(l)(D).

What Kartavaya already has that maps onto this

Two existing capabilities meet verified statutory requirements — the cheapest path into two verticals. Medium · synthesis of six claims

  • E-sign / document routing meets the RERA tri-professional withdrawal certification (three signers, per-withdrawal certificates, UDIN-bearing, retained for annual audit) and the triplicate job-work challan.
  • A date engine keyed to transaction events — not org-level fixed dates — meets every deadline found: ITC-04 on the 25th after a turnover-banded period; 26QB within 30 days of the month-end of each deduction; 16B 15 days after that due date; the RERA statement by 30 September; and the s.143 per-challan ageing clock.

Note the shape of that second point. Every deadline in this research is keyed to a transaction, not to a calendar the org configures once. A statute calendar built on org-level fixed dates would miss all five.

The property-TDS calendar, precisely High · 2-1

Two dated obligations per instalment payment, both keyed off the deduction event:

  • Form 26QB — furnished electronically within 30 days from the end of the month in which the deduction is made.
  • Form 16B — issued to the payee within 15 days from the 26QB due date, not from the filing date.

Because deduction occurs at credit or payment, whichever is earlier, each instalment is its own deduction event with its own pair of deadlines. A construction-linked payment plan therefore generates a rolling stream of dated obligations, not one filing.

Caveats that limit every claim above

  • The 70/30 escrow ring-fence was refuted 0–3. This is the single most widely believed RERA fact. Its refutation is not positive evidence that no such requirement exists — it is surprising enough to warrant re-testing rather than acceptance.
  • Both RERA findings come from one authority — HARERA Gurugram, March 2020 — which states on its own face that neither the Act nor the rules prescribe the format. The tri-professional certification generalises (it restates s.4(2)(l)(D) and is independently confirmed by Goa RERA); the exact column set does not. MahaRERA uses Form 1/2/3 plus Form 5. Whether the 2020 direction is still current could not be checked — 403 on the regulations index.
  • The Income-tax Act 2025 took effect 1 April 2026 and renumbered every section cited. 194-IA maps to s.393(1) Table Sl. No. 3(i); the consideration definition to s.402(9). Sources actively disagree on whether the challan-cum-statement is still Form 26QB or now Form 141 Schedule B (and 16B → 132, 26AS → 168) — and the department's own pages are inconsistent, with the AY 2026-27 page still citing 194-IA, Rule 31A and Form 26QB as operative. A real-estate module cannot ship field labels or help text until this is settled. GST-side citations are stable by contrast (Rule 45 unamended since 2021).
  • Source access was degraded throughout. incometaxindia.gov.in returns 403 to automated fetch, CBIC's portal serves a broken TLS intermediate chain, margcompusoft.com serves an incomplete certificate chain, and haryanarera.gov.in/regulations 403s. Several verifications rest on mirrors or secondary corroboration rather than the cited URL itself.
  • Adversarial search did not run everywhere. Multiple verifiers exhausted their search budget, so no contradiction search was performed for the PAN/TAN claim, either HARERA claim, or the DesignOS claim. Those four carry a real, unquantified risk of unfound counter-evidence — part of why three are marked medium.

What to do with this

  1. Treat the statutory half as build-ready and the competitive half as absent. Scope manufacturing and real estate against these findings. Do not put a price or a positioning claim in front of a prospect on the strength of this run.
  2. Re-run narrowly on what failed — travel in full, pharma statute in full, and pricing by a different method. The vendor pages refuted themselves twice; Indian ERP pricing may simply be dealer-quoted rather than list, which is itself a finding worth confirming.
  3. Do not budget one inventory module for three verticals. The decomposition differs in each and the statutory driver differs in each.
  4. Build the date engine against transaction events, not org-level dates. Every deadline found is keyed to a transaction. This is the single highest-leverage piece of shared infrastructure in the whole picture — it serves both evidenced verticals and the existing statute calendar.
  5. Keep the two TDS paths separate from the first line of code. PAN-only for 194-IA/26QB; TAN-bearing for s.195/27Q/ITNS-281. The existing 281 validator is correct and must not be extended to cover property TDS.

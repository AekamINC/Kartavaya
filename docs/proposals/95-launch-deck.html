02 The problem

An Indian firm runs on six tools and a WhatsApp group.

Tasks in one place, invoices in another, payroll in a spreadsheet, client approvals in a thread nobody can find six months later. The compliance work that defines the practice — GST returns, HSN codes, place of supply — lives in whichever tool was cheapest, bolted on as an afterthought by a vendor who has never filed a GSTR-3B.

Nothing joins up

The invoice does not know about the task. The task does not know the client approved. Someone re-keys it.

Everyone sees everything

Payroll and partner billing sit in the same shared drive as the intern's task list, because access control was never the tool's job.

The client is outside it

Approvals happen over email and WhatsApp, so there is no record of who agreed to what, or when.

03 Why the obvious answer fails

The global tools are excellent, and they are the wrong shape.

Asana, Trello, ClickUp and Monday solve project management beautifully for a software team in San Francisco. Dropped into a chartered accountancy practice in Ahmedabad, three things break, and no amount of configuration fixes them.

GST is not a plugin

CGST/SGST/IGST splits, place of supply, HSN and SAC, LUT export invoices and GSTR summaries are not custom fields. They are the work.

The client has no seat

A practice serves clients who must approve things but must never see internal comments, rates or time entries.

Modules, not workspaces

Payroll and invoicing must be closed to most of the firm. Workspace permissions are too coarse to express that.

04 The wedge

GST built in, not bolted on. And the client approves in one click.

Two things a global tool structurally cannot copy without becoming an Indian product, and two things a domestic accounting package cannot copy without becoming a project manager. Kartavaya is the overlap.

Compliance is native

HSN and SAC codes, the CGST/SGST/IGST split, place of supply, export invoices under LUT, GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B summaries. A tax invoice missing its GSTIN is flagged before it can be sent.

The client needs no account

Send a deliverable for approval and the client gets a link — no signup, no software. They approve or decline with a reason and it lands back on the task. Internal comments stay internal.

06 How access works

Payroll stays closed to the people who don't run it.

Access is granted per person, per module — and the sensitive ones are marked as such. Turning a module off revokes access without destroying the history behind it, so a departure or a role change is a switch, not a data problem.

07 Trust

What we can actually promise.

Four things that are true today and that a firm can check inside the product on its first afternoon. Not a roadmap.

GST-compliant invoicing

HSN and SAC, CGST/SGST/IGST, place of supply, export invoices, GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B summaries.

Every action is logged

Who changed what and when — including platform support access to your workspace. The audit trail is readable by your admins, not only by us.

One client cannot see another

Client accounts see only the projects shared with them. Internal comments, time entries and rates are never exposed.

Access is per person, per module

Sensitive modules are granted individually, and revoking one preserves its history.

08 Commercial model

Four tiers, priced per firm.

Tiers differ by how much AI a firm uses. A credit is one AI request against your own data — drafting a reply, summarising a ledger, pulling a lead out of an email. Everything else is unmetered: tasks, invoices, payroll runs, client approvals and users are not counted.

Basic

A small practice getting off spreadsheets. Priced per user.

Starter

A single-office firm running tasks, invoicing and approvals.

Growth

Multiple partners, client portal in daily use, payroll on.

Scale

Several offices, heavier AI use, higher document volume.

Figures are deliberately absent

No rupee prices and no credit allowances appear in this deck, matching the standing rule on the landing page. The reason is not secrecy: the live catalogue currently holds three different credit allowances for the same tier in three tables, and the price column mixes two incompatible unit conventions. Publishing a precise figure to the audience most likely to hold you to it is the one thing worth avoiding. Resolve the catalogue, then quote. Until then: priced per firm against headcount and volume.

09 Market

Indian practices first. Indian businesses next.

Beachhead — CA and accounting firms

Where GST, client approvals and per-module access all bind at once. The sharpest fit, and the market that refers within itself.

Adjacent — agencies and consultancies

Same client-approval and invoicing shape, less compliance depth.

10 Go to market

Invite-only, and referral-led by necessity.

There is no public sign-up, and that is a decision rather than a gap. It also happens to be the only lawful shape for this market.

  • ICAI rules bar soliciting professional work from prospective clients by email. The audience is largely chartered accountants, so cold outbound is not a channel available to this product. Anyone promising an email-led pipeline into this market has not read the rules.
  • That leaves three channels: referral from firms already using it, organic search for the moment a referred prospect checks you are real, and directory presence for comparison queries.
  • Which makes the website load-bearing. A referral that ends at a search result for the wrong product, or at a demo button with no destination, is a referral lost at the last step.

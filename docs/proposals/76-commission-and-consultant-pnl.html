Consultant figures and commission

Weekly, monthly, quarterly, YTD and yearly turnover, gross profit and margin per person — and the commission computed from them. This is the design, the two migrations that make it possible, and the finding that comes first.

Proposal 76 · branch staging · every figure below measured read-only against the live database on 2026-08-21

The finding, not a caveat

Turnover per person is not computable. Gross profit is not computable at all. Commission cannot be paid on either.

Not "incomplete", not "needs cleaning up". There is no column anywhere in this database that has ever recorded who sold anything, and no line that has ever recorded what anything cost. The report the owner asked for cannot be built from the record as it stands, and no backfill can fix it — there is nothing to backfill from.

What follows is: exactly how bad it is, the two migrations that make the answer possible from the day the write paths change, and what the two report pages print in the meantime. They print the words not attributable and not recorded, on every line. They do not print ₹0.

Cost — nowhere to put one

Across every order line and every invoice line in the database, the keys present are: description, hsn_code/hsn, sac_code/sac, unit, qty/quantity, rate, discount_pct, gst_rate, gst_amount, amount/line_total, product_id. There is no cost key, in either table, on any line. ganit_products.cost_price exists and is filled on 2 of 106 products — and it is the wrong source in any case: a product's cost today is not what it cost when the invoice was raised, so reading it retrospectively re-prices last year's gross profit every time procurement changes a number.

The second blocker, which was not in the brief

staging.manav_employees.user_id is filled on 0 of 98 rows, and 0 employee email addresses match any row in public.users. 98 employees, 32 users, no edge between the two sets at all.

This matters because a commission scheme belongs on the employee (an HR fact) while revenue attributes to a user (a login). Even with both migrations applied and both write paths shipped, a scheme cannot be joined to revenue for a single person until somebody who knows which login belongs to which employee fills that column in.

This is deliberately not automated. Matching on name or on email is a guess, and a guess that attributes one person's turnover to another person's commission is the most expensive mistake this design could make.

2 · The one rule the whole design turns on

A zero is a claim. "Not attributable" is the truth.

SUM(...) over zero attributed invoices returns 0. Rendered under a turnover heading beside a person's name, ₹0.00 is a sentence about that person: they sold nothing. Today that sentence is false for every person in every organisation, because nobody ever wrote down who sold anything. And it is a convincing lie — right-aligned, two decimal places, every appearance of a measurement.

So there are four distinct absences and each one prints its own words:

ReadsMeansFixed by
not attributableNo document in the period records who sold it. Migration 184's column, once the write paths fill it.
not recordedDocuments exist and are attributed, but carry no cost. The cost_price line key, once the write paths set it.
no scheme recordedNobody has said whether this person is on commission. HR recording one, in migration 185's table.
not on commissionA scheme exists and says no. A recorded fact, not a gap. Nothing — this is an answer.

And exactly one zero is allowed: below the threshold. A person who sold ₹9L against a ₹10L threshold is owed ₹0.00, and that is a fact they can check. It is computed and printed as a number, because it is one.

These rows OVERLAP and must not be added together: month to date sits inside quarter to date, which sits inside the financial year. Every period is anchored on 2026-03-31, the last day of the range this report was run for — not on today — so re-running the same report gives the same figures. Weeks start on Monday. Quarters and the year are FINANCIAL (1 April to 31 March, FY 2025-26), never calendar.

Three decisions inside that table

  • There is no footer. The rows overlap, so a column of them adds up to nothing — and a "Total" line under them would be a meaningless number somebody would quote.
  • The year is the Indian financial year. A calendar year-to-date would restate every figure on 1 January, three months out of step with the accounts, the TDS statements and every target the firm sets. Quarters are FY quarters (Apr–Jun, Jul–Sep, Oct–Dec, Jan–Mar) for the same reason — those are the quarters this firm files on.
  • Every period is anchored on the window's last day, never on the clock. A report re-run for a past period must give the same figures. Anchoring on today is how "month to date" silently becomes this month on a report about last April.

This is not a profit and loss account, and must never be called one

Ganit keeps no double-entry ledger, no journal and no chart of accounts. Nothing here deducts overhead, salary, premises or tax, and nothing is a statutory figure or the basis for one. "Gross profit" is a contribution figure — what was billed less the direct cost of what was billed — and the qualification is printed in the table, on both pages, rather than left in a docstring nobody renders.

A column headed "profit" against a consultant's name, in a product with no ledger behind it, will be read as their profit to the firm. It is not that.

Why text and not uuid

public.users.user_id is text — production ids look like user_admin001. Migration 030 converted every created_by / assigned_to / approved_by column in this schema from uuid to text for exactly that reason, after the uuid versions 500'd on every INSERT. vikray_targets.salesperson_id is already text and is the closest existing sibling. No foreign key, for the same reason none of those columns has one: an FK across schemas would make deleting a user fail against every document they ever touched.

The cost key — a convention, not a column

Both line_items columns are already jsonb, so no ALTER is needed. The migration documents the key instead, in a COMMENT that lives in the database:

line_items[].cost_price   numeric — the cost of ONE unit, exclusive of tax, in the
                          document currency, AS AT the moment the line was written.
                          Copied from ganit_products.cost_price at WRITE time, never
                          joined at READ time. ABSENT means NOT RECORDED and must never
                          be read as zero: a zero cost claims a 100% gross margin.

Verified live: zero lines in either table carry a cost_price key today, so nothing is being redefined.

Why there is no backfill, and why there can never be one

All four candidate sources were examined and each rejected:

  • created_by / prepared_by — a data-entry stamp, demonstrably: one account holds 693 of 788 invoices and the two columns contradict each other on 751 rows. Copying it would attribute 96% of the firm's turnover to one person, and pay commission on it.
  • deal_id → assigned_to — the bridge is empty. Six documents attributed, 1,159 blank.
  • contact_id → contact owner — the contact's owner is who manages the relationship now, not who sold the invoice two years ago. Attributing historic commission off a current ownership field pays the wrong person by design.
  • the audit log — holds no per-document authorship over the historic range.

So the column starts NULL on all 1,165 documents and stays NULL until a human or a write path sets it. That is not a gap in the migration; it is the honest state of the record.

6 · Migration 185 — the commission scheme

backend/migrations/185_commission_schemes.sql · written, NOT applied

One new table, staging.manav_commission_schemes, seeded with nothing. One row per version of one employee's arrangement:

ColumnTypeWhat it settles
employee_iduuid On the employee, not the login. A commission arrangement is an HR fact; it survives a change of account and belongs beside the joining date and the designation.
eligibleboolean, default FALSE A recorded fact. FALSE means HR decided no; no row at all means nobody decided. Those render differently and must not be collapsed.
basisturnover | gross_profit What the rate applies to.
rate_percentnumeric(6,3), 0–100 Percent, not a fraction. The unit is in the name so nobody applies a 5 as 500%.
threshold_amountnumeric(14,2), default 0 0 means commission from the first rupee — a real arrangement, not a missing value, which is why it is NOT NULL rather than nullable.
threshold_modeexcess | whole See below. Not in the original brief; added because without it the number is ambiguous.
periodmonthly | quarterly | annual How often it settles, and therefore what "reaching the threshold" means. ₹10L monthly and ₹10L annually are not the same promise.
effective_from / effective_to date / date NULL Half-open [from, to). effective_to is EXCLUSIVE.

Why it is dated, and why half-open

A commission rate changes. When it does, last quarter's commission must still compute on last quarter's rate — otherwise re-running a settled period silently restates what a person was paid, and the firm cannot reproduce the figure on the payslip it already issued.

So the window is modelled exactly the way staging.statute_calendar models a dated statutory fact. The 4% version ends at 2026-04-01 and the 6% version begins at 2026-04-01 — one date, written once. 31 March answers 4%; 1 April answers 6%. Written the other way, with effective_to meaning "last valid day", one day of a person's pay gets either two answers or none, and which it gets depends on row order.

scheme_in_force(schemes, on) takes on as a required argument with no default — "what is this person's rate" cannot be asked without "as of when".

The threshold mode — two very different cheques

"On commission above ₹10 lakh at 5%" is ambiguous in English, and the two readings differ by ₹50,000 on the first rupee over the line:

ModeRate applies to₹12L at 5% over ₹10L
excess (default)the amount ABOVE the threshold ₹10,000
wholethe WHOLE amount, once the threshold is crossed ₹60,000

Both are real arrangements and neither is more correct. A column carrying only a rate and a threshold makes the product pick one silently — and picking excess underpays a person quietly, every period, while picking whole spends the firm's money. So the mode is recorded. This is the one column added beyond the brief, and it is the one worth arguing about.

What the table deliberately does not model

  • Tiered rates ("5% to ₹20L, 8% above"). Needs a child bands table; the upgrade path is additive and this row's rate becomes the last band.
  • Team or override commission (a manager earning on their reports' sales). Needs a hierarchy walk and a policy on whether the manager's own sales also count.
  • Clawback. A period whose credit notes exceed its invoices produces a negative basis; the calculation floors at ₹0.00 rather than returning a negative payment. Recovering an overpayment has notice periods and employment law behind it — it is not arithmetic, and the code will not imply otherwise by returning a number something downstream might pay.
  • Payment. No paid_at, no payroll link. This table says what the arrangement is. What was actually paid is a payroll fact, and conflating the two makes a report into a record of payments never made.
  • Overlap of two closed versions. A partial unique index enforces at most one open version per employee — the realistic mistake. Full non-overlap needs an EXCLUDE constraint and btree_gist, which is available but not installed on this database; installing an extension on the shared production database to guard a mistake nobody has made yet is a bigger change than this file should be. Any residual overlap resolves deterministically (latest effective_from wins) rather than by row order.

8 · What the write path must start capturing

None of this is in the two migrations. It is the work that makes them mean something.

  1. routers/vikray.py — order create/update must set salesperson_id.
  2. routers/ganit.py — invoice create/update must set salesperson_id, and must not default it to the caller. Defaulting to created_by reintroduces the exact fiction this column exists to replace — silently, and with the authority of a real column behind it. An unset salesperson stays NULL.
  3. Both — when a line is written from a product, copy that product's cost_price onto the line. Copy, never join: the line records what it cost then.
  4. An order that becomes an invoice must carry salesperson_id across, or the two registers will disagree about the same sale.
  5. An HR screen to record a commission scheme, and to close the old version when a rate changes rather than editing it.
  6. manav_employees.user_id, filled from records by somebody who knows. Not matched, not inferred.

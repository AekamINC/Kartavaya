01

The primitive — six layers

One class, .lg, on the tokens below. Everything after this section is that class plus a shape.

Most "glassmorphism" is two layers — a translucent background and a blur — which is why it reads as a grey rectangle. Real depth needs six, and the last one is what makes it feel liquid rather than frozen.

/* 1  refraction   */ backdrop-filter: blur(16px) saturate(1.6) brightness(1.06);
/* 2  tint         */ background: rgba(var(--g-tint), var(--g-alpha));
/* 3  specular rim */ inset 0  1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,.46)
/* 4  fresnel      */ inset 0 -1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,.16)
                  inset ±1px 0 0 rgba(255,255,255,.14)
/* 5  elevation    */ 0 8px 28px -8px rgba(12,18,16,.28),
                  0 24px 56px -24px rgba(12,18,16,.32)
/* 6  the liquid   */ ::after — a radial gloss at var(--mx) var(--my)

Layer 6 is the whole trick. A fixed highlight is frozen glass; a highlight that tracks the pointer is liquid glass. It is a pseudo-element with a radial gradient positioned by two custom properties, and about eight lines of JavaScript to write them:

el.addEventListener('pointermove', e => {
  const r = el.getBoundingClientRect();
  el.style.setProperty('--mx', (e.clientX - r.left) / r.width  * 100 + '%');
  el.style.setProperty('--my', (e.clientY - r.top)  / r.height * 100 + '%');
});

Layer 5's negative spread matters: -8px and -24px pull the shadow under the element instead of haloing it, which is what separates elevation from a smudge. And brightness(1.06) in layer 1 is the difference between glass and a grey filter — it lifts what is behind rather than only blurring it.

Everything here retunes from four values — --g-blur, --g-alpha, --g-rim, --g-fres. The Blur switch above writes the first one live. prefers-reduced-transparency flips them to an opaque fill and drops layer 6 entirely.

Toasts — semantic vivid

Your idea, built. Each toast tinted by what it means, with a glass rim that drains in step with the dismiss timer.

Fire one — they stack, drain and exit faster than they entered

Three things this does that a normal toast does not. The rim drains on the same timer that dismisses it, so the bar cannot lie — its duration is interpolated from JS rather than tokenised, which is the documented exemption in check-motion.mjs for exactly this case. The exit is faster than the entrance and travels less, per animations.css. And an Undo slot is built in, because MOTION-SPEC §7.2 says reversible destructive actions get a four-second undo rather than a confirm dialog.

The sticky header — frost that means something

Scroll inside the panel. This is the one case where frost carries information rather than decoration.

Client · Outstanding

Content visibly moving through the header is what tells you the header is a layer above the list rather than part of it. On an opaque header that information is thrown away — which is why proposal 89 found six backdrop-filter rules producing zero pixels: the elements behind them were opaque hex. The fix was never to delete the blur; it was to make the surface translucent so the blur has something to do.

08

Four dialects, one primitive

Apple, Samsung, Google and Nothing all shipped a glass language. Each panel below is the same card, button and chip — only the tokens change. Hover and press each one; they behave differently on purpose.

What each one is actually doing

  • Apple — iOS 26/27. The maximal position: 26px blur, the strongest specular rim, pill geometry, high translucency. It is the most beautiful and the most fragile — iOS 26 shipped a 40% transparency floor, took an American Foundation for the Blind letter in December 2025, and iOS 27 answered in June 2026 by halving the blur radius and pinning text on a denser backdrop. The version here is the corrected one.
  • Samsung — One UI 8.5. The most useful dialect for you, and the one I would actually borrow. Samsung deliberately holds transparency down for readability and lets a visible 1.5px outline define the glass instead of the blur. Their own coverage describes it as turning frosted UI into a dynamic glass effect "with just an outline". Note the button: an extruded lower edge — the "pop-top" — that flattens when pressed. On a data product this reads as glass while keeping text on a 34% fill instead of a 17% one.
  • Google — Material 3 Expressive. Barely translucent at all; its signature is shape morphing. Hover a card and the corner radius animates 28px → 14px on a spring; press a button and it goes 20px → 9px. That is M3's spring motion model doing what a cubic-bezier cannot fake — and it is the one idea here that a CSS transition genuinely can express, because border-radius is animatable.
  • Nothing — Nothing OS. The opposite thesis: transparency without colour. Monochrome, dot-matrix grid, mono type uppercased and letterspaced, 4px hard corners, no specular gloss at all, and red is the only hue permitted — reserved for the destructive action, so colour itself becomes semantic. Desaturating the backdrop (saturate(.1)) is what makes it feel industrial rather than merely grey.

My recommendation for Kartavaya: Samsung's mechanism, Apple's polish, Google's press.

Take the outline-defined glass from One UI — it is the only one of the four that survives a contrast audit on a data product, and it is the answer to "bold frozen glass": the boldness comes from the rim, not from the transparency. Keep Apple's pointer-tracked specular, because that is what reads as liquid rather than frosted. Take M3's radius morph on press, because it costs one transitioned property and makes every button feel physical. Leave Nothing's monochrome, but steal its discipline: one hue, reserved for meaning.

Flip the Ground switch to Cream and watch all four collapse toward each other. Glass dialects are distinguishable only over a ground worth blurring — which is, again, the whole argument.

09

What to hold to

  • Glass never goes behind tabular data. Ninety rupee figures at --row-h: 66px each landing on a different contrast ratio is unfixable. Everything in this kit is chrome, overlay or hero — never the register.
  • One vivid ground per screen. Flip the Ground switch to Cream and watch every component here lose most of its depth. That is not a bug in the components; it is the mechanism. Glass is a relationship between two layers.
  • Text on glass needs a barrier. Every tinted component here keeps its alpha high enough that white clears AA on the tint. Raise transparency past that and you rebuild iOS 26's problem.
  • Count layers, not radius. Set Blur to 28px and the page will still be smooth, because at most a dozen blurred elements exist at once. Eighteen frosted sticky rails on a 1366×768 integrated-graphics laptop is a different story.
  • Infinite animations keep a fixed period. The spinner and the ghost pulse do not multiply by --ix — they put their amplitude on --motion-scale, so at None they go still instead of becoming a strobe.

Set Animations to None and Ground to Cream. Everything stays legible, every affordance still reads, nothing moves. That is the accessibility floor, and it is the reason this kit is built on the tokens rather than on hard-coded values.

What this is. A working component kit, not a mockup — every effect above is CSS in this page's stylesheet, built on Kartavaya's tokens as read from kartavaya-design.css §5 and §7. Lift the .lg primitive and the four --g-* values and the rest follows. Nothing was written to the repository.

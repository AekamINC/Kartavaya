Everything here is the same markup. Flip Design → Shipping in the top bar and the identical screen renders in the cream language that is live today — no mesh, no glass, house shadows, the current palette. Flip back and forth a few times.

That switch is the argument. The bold direction is a token layer, not a rewrite: one ground, one mesh, one glass primitive, one accent pair. Reverting is changing data-design, which is exactly the reversibility you asked for.

What changed, and why. The ground went dark so glass has something to refract and so the accent can actually glow. Display type went to Newsreader 200 at 74px with −.035em tracking — the shipping build never goes above 40px, and scale is most of what reads as premium. Numbers are mono with tabular figures at 36px. The KPI row stagger is 60ms; bars are 38ms apart on --ease-fill; rows fade at 34ms. All on Kartavaya's five-rung ladder, all riding --ix — set Motion to None and the screen goes still, not fast.

What deliberately did not change. The receivables table has no glass, no gradient and no blur behind it. Data stays on a calm ground with a working --row-h and tabular numerals, because ninety rupee figures each landing on a different contrast ratio is unfixable. Semantic pills keep their own hues. That restraint is what lets the rest be loud.

Companion pieces: Motion and Material (the audit and the measurements) and Glass Kit (the components and the four OS dialects).

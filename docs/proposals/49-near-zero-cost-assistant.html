A near-zero-cost assistant that reads like a real one

You asked for two things that usually pull against each other: an AI bill at roughly zero, and a Sahayak that answers the way a good assistant answers. They do not pull against each other here, and this is why. Researched and written 10 August 2026; capacity figures verified against provider documentation the same day.

The finding in one line. At your realistic volume the entire chat load fits inside free inference tiers with several times the headroom — so the money was never the constraint. What makes Sahayak feel poor is three mechanics it does not have, none of which cost anything to add.

1 · What "feels like a real assistant" actually decomposes into

It is not the model. It is five mechanics, and Sahayak has none of the first three:

MechanicWhy it mattersSahayak todayCost to fix
Streaming Words arriving is the difference between "thinking" and "hung". First token in ~300ms beats a complete answer in 3s, every time. none one POST, one JSON body $0
Structure Headings, lists, a bolded lead. Prose in one grey block reads as a machine; the same words shaped read as an answer. none plain text split on blank lines $0
Continuation Ending with what to ask next is most of what makes a chat feel like a conversation rather than a lookup. web path only added 10 Aug $0
Speed Under ~1s to first token, sustained. This is a PROVIDER property, and the fastest providers are the free ones. 2.1s to a complete answer $0 (faster is free)
Model quality Real, but the smallest lever of the five — and the only one anyone reaches for first. flash-lite $0–$11/mo

Near-zero is not aspirational, it is arithmetic. Free capacity covers you to roughly twenty thousand answers a month. Beyond that the paid spill is single-digit dollars, because the spill is priced at flash-lite rates and only the overflow pays.

3 · The one rule that shapes the whole design

A free endpoint is paid for with data. That is what the free tier IS — spare capacity offered as a data-gathering exercise. Google's own documentation is explicit that the Gemini free tier may use submissions to improve its products; OpenRouter forwards to upstream providers whose own training defaults apply.

Sahayak's prompt contains your customers' invoices, deals, salaries and attendance. Sending that to a free endpoint to save a dollar a month would be the single worst trade in this codebase — and it would sit directly against the platform-privacy position that Aekam must not even see client email addresses.

So the router splits by what is in the prompt, not by what is cheap:

LanePrompt containsRoutes toCost
Records lane ledger reads, KB documents, anything org-scoped PAID endpoints only — flash-lite today, or a ZDR-guaranteed OpenRouter route $2.79/mo at 5k
Open lane general questions, Serper results, public web pages free ladder: Cerebras → Groq → OpenRouter :free → paid $0

The split costs nothing to implement: plan and looks_like_org_question already compute exactly this distinction — the route uses them to decide whether to search the web, and would now also use them to decide which provider tier may see the prompt. And the traffic mix works in your favour: most questions are about their own books, and those are short — the expensive long ones are research questions, which are precisely the ones that carry no customer data and can go to the free lane.

4 · The strategy, in the order I would build it

  1. Stream it. An SSE route, sources pushed as the first event so the panel fills while text arrives, charge and store on completion. Every provider under consideration streams over the same protocol. This is the single biggest change to how it feels, and it is free. ~1 day, mostly a streaming sibling in ai_router.
  2. Render it properly. A markdown subset — headings, bold, lists, tables, code — emitted as React elements, never HTML. AnswerBody is elements-only for a reason: a previous build hand-escaped model output into dangerouslySetInnerHTML, which is stored XSS with an AI writing the payload. The renderer keeps that property. ~1 day.
  3. Ask for a better answer. The records prompt still says "be short, specific and literal" and "a short answer that stops is correct" — written to stop invented figures, and it also stops explanation. Rewrite for: lead with the answer, show the figures, then the reasoning, then what to ask next — with the no-invented-figures rule intact. ~2 hours, prompt only.
  4. Add the free lane. Two new provider rows (Cerebras, Groq) and a lane check in _select_providers. Free capacity absorbs every general and web-grounded question; the records lane never touches it. ~half a day. hub_ai_providers is already a table, so the ladder is data.
  5. Parallelise the route. One asyncio.gather over KB search, brand, history and web search — they depend on nothing before them and currently run in single file. ~1 hour, several hundred ms off every answer.
  6. Then, and only then, consider a bigger model. With the first five done, judge whether the answers are still thin. My expectation is that they will not be — but if they are, the whole realistic range costs $3–6 a month and the decision is yours to make on quality alone.

6 · What could go wrong

  • Free tiers rotate and vanish. Free model IDs change month to month. The ladder must fall through silently to paid, and it must never be the case that a free tier disappearing takes the chatbot with it. hub_ai_providers being a table rather than code is what makes this survivable.
  • Cerebras caps free context at 8,192 tokens. Fine for chat and for most web answers; a deep research prompt exceeds it. That prompt goes to the next rung, which is the correct behaviour anyway.
  • Rate limits are per organisation, not per key. Extra keys do not raise them. 30 RPM is generous for one firm and thin for fifty, so the ceiling is per-tenant concurrency, not tokens.
  • Speed is not guaranteed by a benchmark. Your own Qwen logs average 15.5s on content jobs. Before the free lane carries real traffic it needs measuring on real questions — I would ship it behind a flag and compare against flash-lite on the same prompts.

7 · What I need from you

One decision, and it is not about money.

Do you accept the lane rule — that anything containing a customer's records goes to a paid endpoint only, and free inference is reserved for general and web questions? Everything above assumes yes. If you would rather push records through free endpoints too, the bill goes from $1.81 to about zero and I would want that written down as your decision, not mine.

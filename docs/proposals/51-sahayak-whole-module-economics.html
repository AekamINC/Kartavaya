The whole Sahayak module

Proposal 50 priced the chatbot and called it the module. That was wrong. Sahayak is seven surfaces across five vendors, and the chatbot is the cheapest thing in it by three orders of magnitude. This is the corrected analysis. 10 August 2026, from live staging data.

Every scraper in the catalogue is sold below cost.

A credit is worth ₹4 (CREDIT_PRICE_INR). Google Maps Leads charges 5 credits = ₹20 and costs $0.50 = ₹42.50 to run. Business Email Finder charges ₹20 and costs ₹89.25. There is a correct rupee price sitting in the same table — price_inr, ₹350 for that one — and it is not what the customer is charged. The wallet is debited on credit_cost; price_inr is only recorded on the run as billed_inr.

3 · The other number that does not add up

hub_scraper_catalog carries cost_per_run, price_inr AND margin_pct, and the three disagree. Google Maps Leads: cost $0.50 (₹42.50), price ₹70, stated margin 75%. The actual margin at ₹85/$ is 39%. To reach 75% the run would have to cost ₹17.50 — a third of what the column says.

One of the two is wrong. Either cost_per_run is a worst-case at max_results and the typical run is cheaper, or margin_pct was set by hand and never recomputed. This must be settled with an actual Apify invoice before any scraper is repriced — everything below assumes cost_per_run is true, because that is the conservative assumption and it is the one the reconciliation code in admin_orgs.py already compares against.

Scrapers are 89% of your variable cost and the only one that can go wrong.

Text AI across five firms is ₹890 a month. Scrapers at moderate use are ₹12,750. And that is the moderate case. One Growth customer who spends their whole 1,500-credit allowance on Business Email Finder runs 300 of them, costing you ₹26,775 against their ₹15,000 subscription. A single enthusiastic user with a lead-gen target can turn your best customer into your biggest loss, this month, with no warning and no cap in the way.

One UPDATE. It is a 4–18× increase in what a scraper costs the customer, so it must ship with Move 2 or it reads as a price rise rather than as a correction.

Move 2 · Split the wallet in two

WalletCoversBehaviourCost to serve
Included AI chat, content, skills, KB ingest, embeddings Generous fair-use cap nobody honest meets. Not really metered. ₹178/firm/mo
Data & media credits scrapers, images, deep research Real money. Priced at cost + margin, topped up, visibly deducted, capped. ₹2,674/firm/mo

This is the honest shape of the product: talking to your data is free, buying new data is not. It is also easy to sell — every firm understands that a lead list costs money and a question does not.

Move 3 · A hard ceiling per org, per month

A rupee ceiling on the data wallet — say ₹2,000 included on Growth, then top-up. Not a credit balance: a cost ceiling, checked against cost_per_run before the run starts. This is the control that makes the Business Email Finder scenario impossible rather than merely unlikely.

Move 4 · Keep watching Apify's prices

gstin-scraper went up 21.5× without notice; it and the MCA registry are already is_active = FALSE. That discipline was correct. Add to it a monthly reconciliation against the real Apify invoice — the code for it already exists in admin_orgs.py and computes a discrepancy per vendor. Read it once a month.

6 · What this does to the pricing strategy

Proposal 50's advice survives for the AI, and reverses for the data.

  • Chat, content and skills: be generous. Raise the caps as proposed. At ₹178 a firm a month, all the text AI in the module is still a rounding error against a ₹15,000 subscription.
  • Scrapers: never bundle, never discount, always cap. They are a resale business with a 39–86% margin, not a software feature. Price them in rupees, sell them in packs, and let the customer see the meter.
  • Images sit in between — 74% margin, real cost, already metered correctly at 3 credits. Leave them alone.
  • The module story to a customer is stronger this way, not weaker: "ask anything about your own business, free; buy outside data at a published price". That is a sentence a CA firm will understand in one read.

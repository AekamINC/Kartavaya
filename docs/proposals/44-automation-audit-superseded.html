1What is broken, module by module

CRM (Graha) — does nothing

The rejection was right, and it is worse than "4 of 7 actions are no-ops". The whole path is severed in four independent places, any one of which alone would be fatal.

BreakWhat it meansHow verified
The form never collects action config AutomationsTab.jsx:33 initialises conditions:{} and action_data:{} and nothing ever assigns to either. There is no "assign to whom", no "which stage", no "which label", and no condition UI at all. Read. Grep for action_data across frontend/src returns exactly one hit — the initialiser.
Three actions are gated on keys that can never arrive assign_to needs data["user_id"], change_stage needs data["stage"], add_label needs data["label_id"] (graha.py:2008–2048). With action_data permanently {}, all three fall through. Read.
send_notification is offered and has no branch It is in the form's ACTIONS list (AutomationsTab.jsx:21) and the backend's valid_actions. The engine's if/elif chain has no send_notification arm and no else. Read.
The log reports success for work that never happened result = "skipped" if skip else "success" is assigned before the action runs (graha.py:1998). Only an exception changes it. A rule that matched no branch writes result='success' and increments run_count (graha.py:2061). The screen would show a rule running happily, for ever. Read.

And a fifth, which proposal 39 did not reach: even with a perfect config form, one trigger would still be dead on arrival. deal_stage_changed passes only {deal_id, stage} (graha.py:941) — no contact_id — and five of the seven actions are gated on contact_id. On that trigger only change_stage could ever run, and a rule that changes a stage on a stage change is a loop with no guard. Read: call site compared against every action's guard.

A rule cannot even be repaired. There is no PATCH endpoint — only toggle and delete (graha.py:1921, 1939). Fixing a rule means deleting it and recreating it through the same form that writes {}. Read.

Triggers: lead_created (three call sites: manual contact, scraper import, web form), deal_created, deal_stage_changed and activity_created genuinely fire. contact_updated, deal_stale and followup_overdue are accepted by the validator and emitted by nothing. Read: grep for each name across backend/ excluding tests — they appear only in the valid_triggers tuple.

Task automations, the /automations page — actions work, conditions do not

This engine had the treatment its sibling never got: ACTION_CONFIG, configProblems, ActionConfigFields, a builder that refuses to save a rule that would do nothing, and a test-run that reports per-action results. That half is genuinely sound. The other half is not.

BreakWhat it meansHow verified
Two of the three condition fields can never be evaluated The builder offers Status, Priority, Assignee. The event context is {task:{task_id, team_id}, team_id, from, to} — it carries no priority and no assignees. _resolve_field returns MISSING, and matches_filters then refuses to fire. A rule with a condition on priority or assignee never fires, on any event, ever. Status resolves only on status_changed, so on a task_created rule it is dead too. Read, end to end: the two emitters (server.py:3353, 3764), the bulk emitter (tasks_bulk.py:607), _resolve_field and matches_filters. The engine's own docstring admits it at automation_engine.py:145–153.
Six of eight triggers are decoration Only task_created and status_changed are ever passed to fire_automations. field_changed, assigned, due_date_approaching, task_overdue, comment_added and approval_status_changed exist only in the validator's list. Read: every fire_automations call site in the repo, plus a grep proving those six names appear nowhere else in executable backend code.

So the honest summary of this page: choose one of two triggers, no conditions, and the action will run. Everything else the form offers is a promise the engine cannot keep.

3The approved work — the invoice reminder ladder

This is the only thing here you have approved, and the audit changes what building it means. It is not "add a schedule to the existing reminder". The existing reminder points at the wrong person, carries the wrong content, and is delivered to nobody. Six stages, in order, each finishing with something you can see.

L1 · Prove delivery before building anything on top of it. Today every reminder is suppressed and the reminders table says sent. Until that is settled, every stage below would be built on a green light that means nothing. Two parts: make staging.reminders record what outbound actually did rather than the handoff, and decide with you when reminders are allowed to leave staging — noting that staging and production share one database and one sending reputation. This needs your decision, not code.

L2 · Address the customer, not the person who typed the invoice. The recipient becomes the contact on the invoice, with the creator kept as an internal copy if you want one. The data is there: measured, all 210 overdue invoices have a contact, and every one of those contacts has both an email address and a phone number. Nothing has to be collected first.

L3 · The ladder. Steps relative to the due date — my proposal is −3 days (courtesy), day 0, +7, +14, +30, then stop — with one row per step so the history is legible, and dedupe per step rather than the current blanket three days. Stop conditions are the hard part and they are the point: paid, part-paid, cancelled, credit-noted, a promise-to-pay date recorded, or a manual hold. A ladder without a stop is how a firm dunns a customer who paid yesterday. Per-org on/off, and per-invoice suppress.

L4 · The message carries the link and the document. P3 and P5 already built both: the pay page at /i/{token} and services/invoice_email.py with the PDF attached. The ladder reuses them — a third copy of the invoice-sending code is how the payslip sender ended up needing the same two fixes applied twice. It quotes the balance, never the original total, exactly as P5's WhatsApp text learned to.

L5 · Make it visible. A reminder history on the invoice drawer: which step, when, to whom, and whether it was actually delivered or suppressed. This is what makes the whole thing falsifiable, and it is the check the rest of the product does not have.

L6 · WhatsApp as a second channel — last, and only for an org with a connected number. The send path is built; no org can use it. Email first, WhatsApp when there is a WABA to send from.

4Proposals — everything below needs your approval

Ordered so that things which lie get fixed before things which are merely missing. A missing feature is honest; a green log over a no-op is not.

ProposalWhy it is in this order
A Stop the CRM log reporting success for work that did not happen. Smallest change in this document and the one that stops the product from lying. Record success only if a branch ran; add the missing else; name the reason on the log row. Everything else in CRM becomes diagnosable the moment this lands.
B Give the CRM builder the action-config treatment its sibling already has, plus an edit endpoint. "Assign to" must ask whom. This is ACTION_CONFIG / configProblems / ActionConfigFields applied to Graha — the same fix, second engine — plus a real condition UI and a PATCH so a rule can be repaired instead of recreated.
C Task conditions: widen the event context, or remove what cannot be evaluated. Passing the task row instead of two ids makes priority and assignee work. Until then the builder should not offer them. Either is acceptable; offering a condition that silently never matches is not.
D Emit the dead triggers, or take them out of both builders. Six on the task page, three in CRM. Emitting task_overdue and deal_stale means a scheduled sweep, which is real work; taking them out is an afternoon. My recommendation is to remove them now and add them back with their emitters.
E One automations page, under the existing /automations, over one engine. Your rule 4. Today there are two engines sharing a function name and nothing else, and a CRM rule is built on a different screen with a different vocabulary. This is the largest item here and it should come after A–C, because merging two engines while one of them cannot report the truth would hide which half broke.
F Wire auto_send on recurring invoices, or remove the checkbox. Small, and it becomes nearly free once L1 settles whether scheduled mail may leave. Sending is a real decision — it mails a customer on a timer — so it belongs behind the same switch as the ladder.
G Retire the second reminder system, or schedule it. 94 task reminders are due and unsent. Two systems doing one job is how one of them stops being watched. Folding task_reminders into the reminders table would leave one cron, one table, one place to look.

What I am not proposing, and why

No visual flow canvas. Unchanged from proposal 39 and still my recommendation: trigger → conditions → actions is the shape the page already has and the shape a firm can read. If you meant the canvas literally, say so and I will re-plan — it is roughly a quarter more work and it makes simple rules harder to write.

No new triggers before the existing ones tell the truth. Adding deal_stale to a system whose log reports success for no-ops would produce more rules that appear to work.

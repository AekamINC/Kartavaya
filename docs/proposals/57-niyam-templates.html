Niyam Starter Rules

The rules an Indian firm switches on without building anything — clone one, preview who it would reach, arm it. Fifteen of them, ordered by how much damage a mistake could do. Every one is a typed condition and a template: no rule here calls a model, and none can.

Where the order comes from

Arm first (green) — in-app only, one recipient, someone who works here. If it misfires, a colleague sees one wrong notification.

Then (amber) — reaches staff by email or push, or fans out to a few people. A misfire is noise in someone's inbox.

Arm last (red) — reaches a customer. A misfire is a business embarrassment you cannot recall, which is why these wait until their de-duplication windows have been seeded from a dry-run soak. The 331 suppressed reminders already in the database are all of this kind.

Two modules get no rules at all, on purpose

Vetana (payroll) — salary is the most sensitive data in the product and the module already enforces four-eyes approval on a run. An engine that can move a payroll step is a way around a control that exists for a reason.

Pahchan (attendance) — its deletions are DPDP promises with a 72-hour window, and only god-mode reaches the data. Its retention job stays bespoke, outside the engine, exactly as it is.

Refusing to automate something is a design decision worth writing down.

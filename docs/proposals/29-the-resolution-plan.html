How the two bundles get resolved

382 findings across 14 surfaces, of which 99 serious ones survived adversarial re-checking, plus a tablet bundle that landed today. This is the order I would take them in and why that order and not another.

The 99 are not 99 pieces of work. They collapse into eleven packages, because most of them are one defect appearing many times — two table systems shipping side by side accounts for eleven findings on its own, and nine competing bilingual label shapes accounts for five. Fixing the cause fixes the column. Two packages are already running.

Package A — the privilege bugs. This is the only package I would not sequence behind anything else. Six findings, four of them real holes, and they are buried in the middle of a design audit where they read like styling notes:

These are the same class as the two cross-org holes closed this morning, and they were found by a design audit rather than a security one — which is itself the finding.

WAVE 4 · TABLET

Package K — the tablet bundle

Its own rule, and it governs everything: read the window, never the device. Four classes — compact <600, medium 600–839, expanded 840–1199, large ≥1200. The spec is explicit that a 13-inch iPad is 1376pt full screen, 685pt in half Split View and 320pt in Slide Over: same hardware, three layouts, and the narrowest is narrower than a Pixel 8.

Four of its own acceptance checks already pass and I ran them today: Dimensions.get( returns zero hits in mobile/src — the spec calls the frozen-at-launch read "the single most common tablet bug in the ecosystem"; useWindowDimensions is already in use; viewport-fit=cover already ships; and we already gate on (hover: none) and (pointer: coarse) in five places with the two-condition form the spec's Finding 1 argues for, written before that finding existed.

What is left is bounded and the spec says so: a hook, a nav shell in three forms, a PaneHost, a CardList, three lines of app.json. No new screen components — every tablet screen is the phone screen placed in a pane. The web side is three changes only, and the spec forbids porting the native rail to the web.

One conflict, and it is with a decision of ours. §8 Finding 3 fixes the web ladder as: sidebar in flow at ≥1024, overlay with a burger below that, bottom nav at ≤767. We ship a band at 768–1023 that shows the rail and hides the burger — added deliberately, because a 960px screen was otherwise getting the phone treatment. The tablet spec replaces it. I have not touched it. Keep our band or follow the spec is your call, and it is the only place in either bundle where our divergence was a considered improvement rather than an oversight.

3Why this order

  • Privilege bugs first because they are the only findings where waiting has a cost that is not cosmetic. Everything else makes the product worse-looking; these make it wrong about who may do what.
  • Causes before symptoms. Packages B, C and D close 26 findings between them by fixing three things. Running the surfaces first would mean touching the same files again afterwards, and would leave two table systems in place while adding rows to both.
  • Tablet last, and this is the part I would defend hardest. The tablet spec's own estimate is "a navigation shell, a pane host, a hook, and three config lines — the screens do not change." That is only true if the screens are right. Building a pane host around screens that are missing their statutory columns and their status chips just puts two of them on screen at once.
  • Landing and documents are late but not last, because the landing CTA pointing at a UK phone number is the one finding in the set that a prospective customer can see today.

4How each package will actually run

The same shape that has worked all day and has caught things I would not have: recon → build → adversarial verify → repair, with strict file ownership so packages can run in parallel without collision, and split migration number ranges.

  • Recon is read-only and confirms the finding before anything is built. This is not ceremony. Of six defects the bundle still marks Open that I checked by hand this morning, all six were already fixed. The audit's own refute pass killed 5 of 104. Assume the same rate here.
  • Verifiers are told to fail the work. Today that found a prop called and never declared, so every threaded reply posted successfully and then said "Failed to send" — one hit in the tree, no test caught it.
  • Migrations get written, never applied. One staging schema and production writes to it too, so applying is your call. That means every feature must also work with its column absent, which is production's actual state, and each agent has to test that path rather than the happy one.
  • Agents may refuse a brief and say why. One did today: I told it to delete three constants because the handover says to, and it declined — the handover's premise was that the log would carry thread replies, and list_messages still filters them out, so deleting them would have reopened a real defect. That refusal was worth more than compliance.

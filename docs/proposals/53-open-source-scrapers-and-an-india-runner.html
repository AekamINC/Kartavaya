Open-source scrapers, and a thin runner in India

The repos worth building on, the ones worth avoiding despite their star count, and a serious look at a small box in India doing the work. 10 August 2026. Companion to proposal 52.

Stars are the worst reason to pick one of these. The two most starred projects on the list are a browser driver (which is not a scraper) and an AGPL product whose open-source edition exists to sell you the hosted one. License, maintenance and what it actually does when a site blocks you matter more, and they are what the verdict column below is based on.

1 · The repos

Scrapy~62k The classic Python framework. Scheduling, retries, pipelines, throttling. BSD only at volume excellent and heavy. Its value is orchestration we do not need yet — our jobs are one page at a time, triggered by a user.
Crawlee~20k Node. The only OSS option with built-in fingerprint rotation and anti-detection. Apache 2.0 note the author it is Apify's own library. Genuinely good, and leaving Apify by adopting Apify's framework is worth naming out loud. Also Node, and our backend is Python.
SearXNG~20k Self-hosted metasearch over 70+ engines, with a JSON API. The obvious "free SERP" answer. AGPL (we would run it, not ship it) not for production — see below.

SearXNG is the trap on this list, and it is the one you would naturally reach for. It is free, it is self-hosted, it does search. But it works by querying Google and Bing on your behalf, so it inherits their rate limits without inheriting anybody's contract. Production reports are consistent: upstream throttling, results going empty at 2am, permanent maintenance.

Serper costs $0.001 per search and has 2,500 free a month. Replacing a paid API that works with a free one that intermittently returns nothing, to save ten paise a search, is a bad trade in a product a customer is paying ₹15,000 a month for. And there is live litigation between Google and SERP-scraping vendors, which is a reason to be a customer of one rather than to become one.

2 · What I would actually build with

Crawl4AI + Playwright, behind the provider column from proposal 52.

  • Apache 2.0 both, no licence conversation, no commercial agenda.
  • Python, which is what the backend is. Crawlee would mean a Node service.
  • Crawl4AI's output is markdown built for LLMs — which is what Sahayak's answer path already consumes, so a scraped page and a Serper result can arrive in the same shape.
  • It covers exactly the six rows proposal 52 wants moved: contact finders, website extraction, the simple listing pages. It does not magically defeat Instagram or LinkedIn, and nothing on this list does.

3 · A thin runner in India

You asked about a small on-prem box in India. It is a better idea than it first looks, for a reason that has nothing to do with hardware.

Why an Indian egress IP genuinely matters

  • Indian sites behave differently for Indian traffic. JustDial, IndiaMART, MCA, GST portals, Amazon.in and Flipkart all serve, rate-limit and sometimes block by geography. Today every request leaves from Railway in Singapore or from Apify's US proxies. An Indian IP is not an optimisation, it is often the difference between a page and a captcha.
  • Residency. The database is in Singapore and settled (2026-07-27). But a runner that fetches and processes in India, and only sends the extracted rows onward, is a real answer to a CA firm asking where their prospect data is handled.
  • Cost. A scrape that costs ₹44 through Apify costs electricity on a box you already own.

Start with A, keep B in your pocket. ₹200–500 a month buys an Indian egress IP and a place to run Crawl4AI, and it is reversible in an afternoon. Move to B only when you see Indian sites blocking the datacentre IP — which you will find out within a week of trying, and not before.

C is a real product one day — "your data never leaves your office" is a genuine enterprise wedge in India — but not with zero funding and five clients. It is a sales promise that costs support hours forever.

The architecture, which is the part that matters

Whatever the box is, it must be a thin runner: it takes a job, fetches, extracts, posts rows back, and holds nothing.

  • It never touches the database. It calls an authenticated endpoint with its results. A scraper box is the most exposed thing you will own — it executes untrusted HTML by design — and it must not hold a credential that can read a tenant.
  • It pulls work, it does not accept it. Long-poll a queue rather than opening a port. This makes CGNAT irrelevant, which is what kills most home-broadband plans.
  • It is one more provider value. Proposal 52's driver column already gives us inhouse; the runner is just where that driver executes. Same catalogue card, same credit price, same cap.
  • Apify stays as the fallback. Runner down, blocked, or slow → the job falls through to the Apify actor. You get the cheap path when it works and the reliable path when it does not, and the customer never learns which one ran.

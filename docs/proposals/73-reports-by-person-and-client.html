The identity break: two person namespaces that never touch

staging.manav_employees.user_id is NULL on 98 of 98 rows, and not one of the 98 employee emails matches a login email either. The product holds two disjoint populations: 32 logins carrying tasks, time, deals and approvals; and 98 personnel files carrying 1,659 punches, 242 leave requests and 1,095 payslips.

A single report combining “tasks done” with “attendance and leave” is impossible for every one of the 98 employees. The linking feature is fully built — the endpoint, its migration and its test all ship. Nobody has ever pressed the button. This is data entry, not engineering.

The measurement traps — read before ranking anyone

This product has already shipped one wrong person-level metric. /reports counted time-entry rows and labelled the column tasks_done, so whoever logged their week in the most separate entries won; then a PDF crowned the top of that list “CHAMPION OF THE PERIOD” by name, in a document a schedule can mail to a free-text address list. Removed on 19 August. Everything below is still standing.

  1. Tasks have no size. estimated_minutes is set on 127 of 735. One row can be “reply to email” or “close the audit”. A task count is a count of rows.
  2. Shared tasks. 111 tasks have more than one assignee. Count once per assignee and say so on the report; splitting credit 1/n invents a fraction the data does not contain.
  3. Closer is not assignee. Of 240 completions, 52 were closed by someone not on the task, and 70 were self-created and self-closed. Report both columns and show the divergence.
  4. Allocation is not effort. Two people hold 76% of Aekam's completions; one rep holds 85% of assigned deals. A per-person number without “how much was routed to this person” beside it reads as merit.
  5. 45 tasks are done with no completion date and belong to no period. Carry them as done_undated or a person's total will simply look short and nobody will know why.
  6. 265 of 735 tasks are archived. Inherit a picker's default and you erase whole engagements from somebody's record.
  7. Cycle time is unmeasurable. There is no status-transition history anywhere. Only created→done lead time exists.

Therefore: no sorting by a person-metric descending, no rank numbers, no top or bottom performer. Alphabetical by name is the honest default. Every trap above says the ordering is unreliable, and a ranked list is the one thing a reader believes without checking.

The cheapest genuine unlocks are not migrations

  1. A client dropdown on the expense form. The column has existed since migration 019, the API accepts contact_id at routers/ganit.py:1491, and frontend/src/pages/ganit/ExpensesTab.jsx never sends it. Write path only.
  2. Backfill 98 manav_employees.user_id values. A one-time mapping through an endpoint that already exists. It unblocks every employee-cost question at once.
  3. A cost key inside line_items at write time. Verified across all 389 order lines and 1,341 invoice lines: zero occurrences of any cost key. line_items is schemaless JSONB, so this needs no migration — but ganit_products.cost_price is populated on 2 of 106 products, so there is nothing to snapshot yet.
  4. tasks.client_id — the expensive one, and the only one that actually buys profitability. Price it as a behaviour change: every fee-earner, on every task, forever.

What such a report must say on its face

Ganit has no ledger, no journal and no chart of accounts. Any figure here is a contribution figure, and to a chartered accountant “profit” means something audited and reconcilable. Four rules, none of them a footnote:

  • The title must not contain the word profit. Ship it as “Client revenue and recorded costs”.
  • A cost column that reads “not recorded”, never ₹0. Zero cost renders every client at 100% margin — a claim nobody made.
  • No margin percentage at all while the cost denominator is zero. It is the single most quotable wrong number the screen could emit.
  • Label which revenue it is — invoiced, recorded as received, or bank-confirmed. Only 23 clients and 30% of the rupees are bank-confirmed, and there is no gateway: bank reconciliation is the only external proof that exists.

The spine is built and unplugged at both ends

report_section() runs a report definition and returns {label, data: rows}; render_report_html() turns that into letterhead HTML, and CSV, XLSX and PDF already work. The entitlement rule gates on what a report reads, not just its module, so a cross-module join cannot slip past a grant.

But sections_for and report_section are called by nothing in production; layout validation rejects any item that is not a registered metric, so a section cannot even be saved through the API; and the only report definition in existence, receivables ageing by party, is therefore unreachable by any route.

Worse than previously documented: routers/analytics.py:1226 builds w["metric"] over every widget carrying data, and that payload is constructed before the format branch. A section in a layout 500s on all four formats, not only JSON. The fix is w.get("metric") and a null filter.

Late arrivals and geofence exceptions are computable right now. The schema moved and the absence reasons did not.

Three things I would not do

  • Do not rank people. Not by task count, not by activity events, not by anything in this schema. The last time this product put a person at the top of a list, it was counting the wrong table.
  • Do not build the employee report in routers/reports.py. It resolves no org, its membership check reads one of the two membership tables, and it short-circuits for all eight platform role codes — which would hand every Aekam account every customer's employee data.
  • Do not fold attendance into a task report without gating the whole report on pahchan. Attendance is biometric data under the DPDP notice; folding it into a core-module surface launders it past a gate that never sees it.

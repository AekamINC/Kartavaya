Getting off Apify where it is not earning its price

You are right that a middleman who can double a price overnight is a bad place for your cost structure to live. But the answer is not to leave Apify — it is to stop paying Apify for things that are free from the source. 10 August 2026.

Six of nineteen scrapers should not be Apify calls at all.

Four of them have an official first-party API that is free or near-free, and one of those four you are already paying for and already have wired. Move those six and Apify keeps only the hostile targets — which is exactly what a price-volatile middleman is worth paying for.

1 · The one that should embarrass us

Google Search Results — Apify, measured today: a floor of $0.50 per run (₹44) for up to 100 results, about to be repriced to 19 credits.

Serper — the search vendor already integrated for Sahayak, already keyed, already inside its free tier: $0.001 for the same 100 results, with 2,500 searches a month free.

That is roughly 500× the price, for the same rows.

Two catalogue entries — Google Search Results and SEO — Google Search Results & Keywords — are paying a scraping middleman to do something the product does natively, for free, on another screen.

3 · Why keeping Apify for the rest is the right call

The nine that stay are the ones where the cost is not the compute — it is proxy rotation, anti-bot defeat, and someone else standing between you and a platform's terms of service. Building those in-house means:

  • A proxy bill that behaves exactly like the one you are trying to escape — residential proxies are priced per GB and repriced at will, and you would own the blocking problem as well.
  • Direct legal exposure. LinkedIn is the sharpest case: scraping profiles breaches their terms, and the exposure sits with whoever makes the request. Today that is Apify. In-house, it is Aekam — and you are selling to accounting firms, who care about exactly this.
  • Permanent maintenance. Instagram changes shape every few months. Nineteen scrapers is a full-time job; nine that somebody else maintains is a line item.

Apify's real problem is not that it is expensive. It is that it repriced silently. That problem is now solved — the daily price watch shipped today catches a move the next morning and deactivates anything that jumps 3× rather than passing it on.

4 · Two caveats I will not paper over

Google Places is not a free lead list. The Places terms restrict how long you may cache and whether you may redistribute place data. A lead-generation feature that stores results in graha_contacts forever may not be a permitted use, whatever it costs. Worth a proper read before we move it — the ₹44-per-run saving is not worth a terms problem on the one Google service the whole product touches.

Email verification is the hard half. Extracting an address from a website is a regex. Deciding whether it accepts mail is an SMTP probe against someone else's server, and doing it badly gets our sending domain reputation-flagged — which would damage Prachar, an unrelated module. If we build the finder in-house I would ship it as "found on the site", not "verified", and leave verification to a vendor.

5 · What I would build, and in what order

  1. A provider column on the catalogue. One field — provider ∈ {apify, serper, youtube, meta, places, inhouse} — and a driver per value behind the existing run endpoint. The customer sees the same card and the same credit price; only where the rows come from changes. This is the piece that makes every later move a config change.
  2. Serper driver. Kills the two Google Search rows. No new vendor, no new key, no approval to wait for. Half a day, and it is the biggest single saving on the list.
  3. YouTube driver. Free at our volume. Needs a Google Cloud project, which we have.
  4. Meta Ad Library driver. Free, but start the identity verification now — it takes one to two weeks and nothing else is blocked on it.
  5. In-house contact finder. Fetch, parse, extract. Ships as "found", not "verified".
  6. Places — only after somebody reads the caching terms.

What this is worth. The six moved rows are among the most used in the catalogue and their Apify cost falls from ₹3–44 a run to roughly zero. More importantly, your exposure to a surprise price rise drops from nineteen third-party actors to nine — and the nine that remain are the ones where you are genuinely buying somebody else's problem rather than their markup.

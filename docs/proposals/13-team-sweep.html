Eight identical teams, same name, same day, same two members, zero tasks. That is not somebody creating eight teams. That is a create-team path firing repeatedly — most likely a double-submit with no idempotency, or a retry loop. The two audit teams are the same story a week later, differing only by "&" versus "and", which reads like one person retyping after the first attempt appeared to fail.

Deleting the rows tidies the symptom. Worth a look at the create-team handler either way — if it still has no guard, this will happen again.

The decision

My recommendation: delete the 18, keep the 165. The 18 grant access to teams that belong to nothing, contain nothing, and were created by what looks like a bug. There is no scenario where removing them loses work — there is no work.

It is also reversible. The rows are snapshotted into staging.team_members_removed_backup before the delete, exactly as the three demo-org rows were, so restoring is one INSERT.
What actually runs, if you say yes: 
BEGIN;
INSERT INTO staging.team_members_removed_backup (reason, member_id, team_id, email, user_id, role, status, created_at)
SELECT 'orphan teams sweep 2026-08-05', member_id, team_id, email, user_id, role, status, created_at
  FROM public.team_members tm
  JOIN public.teams t ON t.team_id = tm.team_id
 WHERE t.org_id IS NULL
   AND tm.user_id IN (SELECT user_id FROM staging.user_roles WHERE org_id IS NULL)
   AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM public.tasks x WHERE x.team_id = tm.team_id);

DELETE FROM public.team_members tm
 USING public.teams t
 WHERE t.team_id = tm.team_id
   AND t.org_id IS NULL
   AND tm.user_id IN (SELECT user_id FROM staging.user_roles WHERE org_id IS NULL)
   AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM public.tasks x WHERE x.team_id = tm.team_id);
COMMIT;
The NOT EXISTS on tasks is the safety rail: if any of these teams turns out to hold work between now and running it, its rows are left alone rather than deleted on the strength of a count I took earlier today.
And the honest caveat. This is data tidying, not the fix. Even with all 18 gone, the code still returns every team in the database to a platform account with no org row — that is item 1 of the plan and it is what actually closes the leak. Deleting these rows without that fix removes a symptom and leaves the cause.

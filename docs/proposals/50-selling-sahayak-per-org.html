Selling Sahayak per organisation

Unit economics at five firms and fifty-odd users each, what to charge, and how to run it with no funding behind you. Written 10 August 2026 against live staging data (plans, credit_prices, hub_ai_logs) and current market pricing.

AI inference is 0.15% of the revenue it earns.

Five firms, fifty-five users each, on expected usage: ₹120 a month in model cost, total, across all five. At Growth pricing those five firms bill ₹75,000 a month. Sahayak is not a cost centre and pricing it as though it were is the mistake to avoid.

A Growth customer paying ₹15,000 a month hits "out of credits" three weeks in — on a feature whose true cost to serve them was ₹19. They do not experience that as a quota. They experience it as the product breaking, they raise a ticket, and one support conversation costs you more than a year of that firm's inference. You are rationing something that is effectively free, and paying for the rationing in churn.

4 · What the market is doing

Two currents, and they point in different directions for you:

  • Pure per-seat pricing is collapsing — down from 21% to 15% of SaaS vendors in twelve months, while hybrid (base fee + variable) went from 27% to 41%. Roughly three in four vendors repriced in the last year.
  • AI application margins are bad — ICONIQ puts application-layer AI gross margins at 50–60% against classic SaaS's 80%, and some AI-native products ship near 17%. Everybody is being squeezed by inference.
  • Your Indian price ceiling is real — CA practice tools sell at ₹100/user/month (Turia) and ₹7,500–17,000 per year for 10–40 users (QwikCA). Your ₹10,000–20,000/month plans are already priced far above the category, which means you are selling on value, not on price — and an AI assistant is exactly the thing that justifies that gap.
You are the exception to the margin story, and you should exploit it. Everyone else's AI margins are 50–60% because they run large models on long contexts. Yours are 99%+ because a Sahayak answer is 1,400 tokens on a flash-lite-class model with Serper doing the searching. That is a structural advantage in a market where your competitors will have to meter. Do not voluntarily give it up by copying their credit meters.

5 · The strategy — four moves

Move 1 · Stop selling credits. Sell answers, generously.

Nobody understands a credit. Everybody understands "3,000 questions a month". Keep the ledger internally — it is good engineering and it is your cost attribution — but express the entitlement in answers, and set it high enough that no honest customer ever meets it.

Even at 100% consumption of the raised caps, Sahayak costs under 2.5% of the plan fee. The cap exists to stop a runaway script, not to price the feature.

Move 2 · Sell it standalone at ₹3,000/org/month

For firms that want the assistant but not the whole platform — and as the upsell path for anyone on Free. Includes 3,000 answers, ₹1 per answer beyond it (fifty times cost, and still cheap enough that nobody argues). At 55 users that is ₹55/user/month, which sits comfortably against a market that pays ₹100 for a whole practice tool, because this is an addition rather than a replacement.

Per-org, not per-seat. Per-seat AI pricing punishes the firm for giving access to junior staff, which is precisely the adoption you need in year one. A firm that puts Sahayak in front of 55 people and finds it useful renews. A firm that bought 10 seats and rationed them does not.

Move 3 · Client-by-client differentiation, in three tiers

Move 4 · Meter only what is actually expensive

From proposal 48: 79% of every rupee you have spent on AI is images — one generated image costs more than 150 chat answers. Deep-research runs on a premium model cost ~₹4 each. Those two deserve a meter. Chat does not.

Your cost is fixed, not variable — and that is the whole strategy. Adding the second, third and fifth client costs you almost nothing: the model bill moves by ₹19 a firm and the platform bill does not move at all. Your measured Railway driver is always-on compute for two environments, not traffic. So one paying customer covers the entire run-rate, and every one after that is close to pure margin. With $1,000 sunk and no funding, that is the only number that matters: break-even is one client, not ten.

7 · Operating rules for a zero-funding launch

  • Never sign a fixed AI commitment. No reserved capacity, no annual model contracts. Everything usage-priced and free-tier-first (proposal 49). Your costs must be able to fall to near zero in a bad month.
  • Sleep staging. Two always-on environments is your largest real bill and half of it serves nobody at 3am.
  • Price annually where you can. ₹1,62,000 up front from one Growth customer is eighteen months of runway at your burn. The annual prices are already in plans at a 10% discount — sell them.
  • Cap per user, not per org. A 60-answers-a-day per-user cap stops the one runaway script that could actually cost money, and no human will ever touch it. Org caps punish adoption; user caps punish abuse.
  • Fix the $0 logging first. Four configured models have no price entry, so direct-Gemini calls log ₹0. Before you price anything on per-org cost data, make that data true.
  • Support is your real COGS. At these margins the only variable cost that matters is your own hours. Every "out of credits" ticket is worth more than a year of that customer's inference — which is the argument for Move 1 restated as a cash number.

8 · Risks

  • The margin holds only while answers stay short. 1,400 tokens is today's shape. Add long research runs by default, or a bigger model on every question, and 99% margin becomes 90% — still fine, but the arithmetic above stops applying. Re-measure if the answer shape changes.
  • Free tiers rotate. The open lane in proposal 49 must fall through to paid silently, or a provider's policy change becomes your outage.
  • Data residency will be asked about. CA firms handle client financials. BYOK (Move 3) is the answer that costs you nothing; have it ready before the question rather than after.
  • Per-org cost visibility is currently unreliable. If you promise a customer a usage report, build it on corrected logs.
  • GST. SaaS in India carries 18%; quote exclusive and let the invoice say so. Your customers are accountants — they will notice.

9 · What I need from you

Three decisions.

  1. Raise the caps? 500 / 1,500 / 5,000 credits → 4,000 / 12,000 / 40,000 (i.e. 2,000 / 6,000 / 20,000 answers). One UPDATE on plans. My recommendation: yes, before the first real customer meets the current ceiling.
  2. Standalone Sahayak at ₹3,000/org/month, 3,000 answers, ₹1 overage? Or a different number — but per-org, not per-seat.
  3. BYOK as the enterprise tier? It is the cheapest answer to the hardest objection you will get, and it needs a key field on the org and a provider row. Small build, large door.
